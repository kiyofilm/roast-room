# Stories: Danny Meyer

Built from the batch notes on six unique sources (S005 is the same episode as S001). 12 stories, most used first, each with its lesson. Paraphrase only. All are restaurant stories. Tabla's closing date conflicts across sources and is recorded below without picking one.

1. **The two sets of keys, 1995.** Soon after Gramercy Tavern opened, the bookkeeper kept a smiley key for Union Square Cafe and a frowny key for Gramercy Tavern because of how guests were treated. He called an all-staff meeting and named hospitality for the first time. Lesson: standards slip when the owner is not at the door, so name and teach them. (S007)
2. **James Beard and Zagat.** The morning after Union Square Cafe won the Outstanding Service award he felt like a fraud. Zagat ranked it about 10th for food, 12th for service and 65th for decor, yet it was the favorite restaurant in New York. Lesson: something Zagat did not measure drove the love, and he called it hospitality. (S002, S007)
3. **Seth Godin's complaint at Maialino.** Godin gave feedback as a gift and left the fix to Meyer. Lesson: take the complaint, own the fix, and write the guest a good next chapter. (S001)
4. **9/11 at Union Square Cafe.** Staff were already making sandwiches for people with no instruction. He ran an emergency restaurant week with 150 restaurants. Lesson: culture works unprompted, and after a shock address the emotional barrier first. (S004)
5. **Reviews that hurt.** A "dot" review and a "Stepford Wives" review, which called the niceness fake and hurt him most. He tells these with humor and does not defend. Lesson: criticism stings but should not change who you are. (S004, S007)
6. **Eleven Madison Park and the room.** It opened as a brasserie; guests said the food did not fit the room, so the point of view changed to fit the architecture. Lesson: listen to guests and to the setting. (S006)
7. **The hot dog cart to Shake Shack.** A cart in Madison Square Park in 2001 drew about 100 people a day, guests asked for it back, and a permanent kiosk followed. He used it to prove hospitality is not only for fancy places. Lesson: test the idea in a cheap setting. (S007, S006)
8. **Tabla.** He kept it open longer than he should have (two years, S001) out of fear, then closed it carefully. Date conflict: 2010 (S002), 2011 (S007). S006 (2012) has him saying he had never closed one in 24 years. S003 (2019) says four closures in 34 years. S001 says he regrets bragging he never closed one. Lesson: closing well is not shameful, and he was wrong about the opening line. (S001, S002, S003, S006, S007)
9. **Hospitality Included and the 2020 reversal.** Servers at North End Grill were cooks who could not afford to cook, which led him to price tips into the menu. Guests accepted it, profits eroded, and he later resumed tipping while paying cooks a revenue share. Lesson: a fair idea still needs the math to hold. (S001, S002, S003)
10. **Grandmother's garden and tomato sauce.** Water the flowers, and a recipe is only as good as its worst ingredient and how you treat it. Lesson: attention and ingredients both matter. (S001)
11. **The 1980 campaign.** Everyone was a volunteer, so only a higher purpose motivated them. Lesson: treat staff as people who could work anywhere. (S001, S007)
12. **Rome tour guide.** At 20 he turned cranky, jet-lagged tourists into happy ones in days. Lesson: early practice in reading what people need. (S001, S007)
