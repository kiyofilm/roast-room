# Frameworks: Danny Meyer

Built from the batch notes on six unique sources (S001, S002, S003, S004, S006, S007). S005 is the auto-caption copy of the S001 episode and counts as the same source. 13 frameworks, most used first. Names are his where he said them; otherwise a short name marked *(unnamed)*. "How to score" lines are guidance for the council built from his own thresholds, not quotes. All of his experience is restaurants and hospitality at scale; none is leased-home rentals. Restaurant dependence is flagged per entry.

---

## 1. Hospitality versus service, the 49/51 split *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Service is whether you delivered the technical promise. Hospitality is how you made the guest feel. Technical delivery earns at most 49 points of 100. Feeling earns the other 51.
- **Parts:** technical delivery (food on time, basics right); emotional delivery (guest felt you were on their side); the origin: Zagat and the James Beard award showed love came from something other than scores.
- **When to use:** Any guest-facing choice, and any argument that a better product or cheaper price is enough.
- **How to score (guidance):** High if the guest would say they felt looked after and the basics also work. Low if the plan only improves the technical side. A guest-feel win with broken basics also scores low, since he says feeling does not rescue a broken promise (S001, S003).
- **Quote:** "service and product only gets you about 49% of the way there. I would say that hospitality is worth 51 points if you're trying to get 100 on your test." (S002)
- **Sources:** 4 (S001, S002, S003, S007)
- **Rental flag:** His proof is a staffed dining room. For a rental the feeling comes through the listing, messages, setup, cleanliness and how a problem is handled (inferred from S002, S003).

## 2. Five stakeholders, in order, as a virtuous cycle *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Every business has five stakeholders. His order: employees, guests, community, suppliers, investors. It is a cycle, not a totem pole: happy employees make happy guests, and happy investors are an outcome, not the input.
- **Parts:** show each stakeholder first that you are on their side; employees first; investors last as the result.
- **When to use:** A choice that trades cost against people (pay, cleaner, hired help, a supplier, a landlord).
- **How to score (guidance):** Ask which of the five gets worse and whether the plan only helps the last one. A plan that gains for investors by hurting employees or guests scores low.
- **Quote:** "We put our customers second, communities and where we work, third. And fourth, our suppliers, and fifth, our investors." (S003)
- **Sources:** 2 (S001, S003)
- **Rental flag:** Read the landlord and neighbours as the "community" and suppliers (inferred from S001, S003). He does not say this.

## 3. Hospitality quotient: hire for emotional skill *(his term: "HQ")*
- **What it is:** His hiring screen. Six emotional skills at a high level, and someone happiest when making another person feel better. It has nothing to do with whether they can cook.
- **Parts:** the six traits he confirms in S001: kind eyes, curiosity, work ethic, empathy, self-awareness, integrity; he would add loves to win. S002 does not list the six.
- **When to use:** Picking a cleaner, co-host or helper who touches the guest experience.
- **How to score (guidance):** Technical skill is teachable; attitude is the filter. High if the person visibly cares how the guest feels; low if only the skill fits.
- **Quote:** "people who are happiest themselves when they're making someone else feel better" (S002)
- **Sources:** 3 (S001, S002, S003)

## 4. Acts of thoughtfulness and customizing for the guest *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Lift the sale from money for a product into a hospitality transaction where the guest pays for how they felt. Ask what thoughtful acts you did and what you customized.
- **Parts:** read what you know about the guest before they arrive; remember preferences; seat or serve to fit them.
- **When to use:** Deciding what extras, touches or message habits are worth the cost.
- **How to score (guidance):** High if the touch is specific to this guest and cheap to repeat. Low if generic.
- **Quote:** "how did we make you feel? What were the acts of thoughtfulness? What were the things we did to customize that service experience for you so you go, why would I go anywhere else?" (S003)
- **Sources:** 3 (S001, S003, S007). In S001 the practice is ABCD, "always be collecting dots", with nightly reading of reservations. In S007 it is remembering each guest's hot dog toppings.
- **Rental flag:** Reservation reports and a host stand depend on a restaurant. The nearest rental version is guest notes and booking data (inferred from S001, S007).

## 5. Listen to guests, filter the noise, keep the old and add the new *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Start from a sharp point of view, then run it as an ongoing dialogue with guests. Take the constructive feedback, drop the hurtful noise, and give people enough of what they came back for plus enough that is new.
- **Parts:** sharp point of view; listen to guests and to the room itself; tune in the constructive and tune out the hurtful; do not strip what brought them back.
- **When to use:** Reviews, complaints, and any plan to change a property that already earns good reviews.
- **How to score (guidance):** High if the change answers a pattern in what guests say and keeps the reasons they return. Low if it answers one angry review or ignores all feedback.
- **Quote:** "tune in the constructive stuff and tune out the stuff that's just hurtful" (S006)
- **Sources:** 3 (S006, S007, S001). S001 adds the Seth Godin story of a complaint as a gift; S007 shows complaints reshaping plans and criticism as fuel.

## 6. Hospitality is the moat *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Ideas, menus and technical service get copied fast, but how a guest felt cannot be photographed and copied. So each new idea needs a stronger hospitality commitment.
- **When to use:** Worry about competitors, price wars, and copycat listings.
- **How to score (guidance):** High if the plan builds something a rival cannot copy by looking at photos. Low if the edge is something any new listing can match.
- **Quote:** "You can photograph how I cook my chicken, but you can't photograph how the server made you feel when she was delivering it." (S002)
- **Sources:** 3 (S002, S003, S007). S007: "triple down on our core competence, which is how do you make people feel".

## 7. Staff happiness turns into guest experience *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Staff performance is a direct result of how happy they are working there. Letting staff down shows up as letting guests down. Treat staff as volunteers who could work anywhere, and give them a higher purpose than the task.
- **When to use:** Pay, scheduling, how the cleaner is treated, and turnover.
- **How to score (guidance):** High if the plan makes the people who meet the guest better off. Low if it saves money by squeezing them.
- **Quote:** "I still wanted to treat people as if they were volunteering to work here, and I still wanted to give people a higher purpose for their job other than the task itself" (S007)
- **Sources:** 3 (S001, S006, S007)
- **Limit:** He says pay and benefits cost real money in thin margins: "the downward pressure on your pricing ability is great." (S004)

## 8. The 51 percent employee and culture carriers *(his phrase: "51 percenters")*
- **What it is:** Each person is scored out of 100: technical performance at most 49, hospitality performance 51. Do not promote someone with full technical marks who does not carry the culture.
- **When to use:** Deciding who to keep, promote or replace among hired help.
- **How to score (guidance):** Both halves must be present. High technical and low hospitality is not a pass.
- **Quote:** "There’s your technical performance, how well you do the job you’re paid to do. The most points you can get for doing it perfectly is 49." (S001)
- **Sources:** 2 (S001, S002). S002 states the promotion rule.

## 9. Four quadrants of performance (can/can't, will/won't)
- **What it is:** Technical ability on one axis (can, can't), emotional willingness on the other (will, won't). Can and will: celebrate. Can't but will: coach, long time. Can't and won't, and can but won't: short window.
- **When to use:** A helper who keeps missing the standard. Decide coach or exit, and give the warning first.
- **How to score (guidance):** Will beats can. Coach people who will; he says the payoff is loyalty.
- **Quote:** "the wick on my candle is pretty long for someone who will" (S001)
- **Sources:** 1 (S001; the S005 caption copy repeats it). He puts it on locker-room mirrors so nobody first hears it in a firing talk.

## 10. Culture is what you celebrate minus what you tolerate *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** The culture is the sum of wanted behaviors you celebrate minus unwanted behaviors you tolerate. Tolerating bad behavior calls the praise into question.
- **When to use:** A helper who performs but cuts corners, or a standard that slips.
- **Quote:** "the culture you have in your organization is the sum of all the wanted behaviors that you celebrate, minus all the unwanted behaviors that you tolerate." (S001)
- **Sources:** 2 (S001, S002). S002 gives the growth side: culture carriers get the rewards first.

## 11. Water the flowers, not the weeds *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Spend attention on the people already doing great, not on problem people. People turn toward the sun like sunflowers.
- **When to use:** Time split between a star cleaner and a struggling one.
- **Quote:** "our employees are like sunflowers. They will turn wherever the sun is." (S001)
- **Sources:** 1 (S001). He says he spent his first ten years doing the opposite.

## 12. Fix the guest's pain points by design *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** List the moments in the experience you personally dislike and remove them in how it is built. His examples were tipping, waiting for the coat, and a busy signal. The all-in menu price removes the surprise.
- **When to use:** Fees, check-in friction, house rules and anything that surprises the guest at the wrong moment.
- **How to score (guidance):** High if a surprise cost or hassle is removed. Low if it is moved to the bill.
- **Quote:** "there's like three points in a meal that I just don't enjoy." (S002)
- **Sources:** 2 (S002, S003). S003: "So the menu price covers everything."
- **Limit:** He later went back to tipping in 2020 and says the no-tip model eroded profit (S001).

## 13. Name it, teach it, be intentional *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Giving something a name lets you teach it and do it on purpose. In 1995, when he could not be at every front door, he named hospitality for the first time at an all-staff meeting.
- **When to use:** Spreading a standard to people who are never in the room with the owner.
- **Quote:** "once you can name something, then you can teach it, and then you can be intentional about it." (S002)
- **Sources:** 2 (S002, S007)
- **Rental flag:** For absent owners this means written standards, templates and checklists (inferred from S002, S007).
