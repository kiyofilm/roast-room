# Positions: Steward

37 topics on questions an STR arbitrage owner faces, built from the World English Bible notes (S001 to S066; batches 01 to 05). Each topic gives a stance, a verbatim quote with Book c:v and source id, and a Context line: command, description, Israel's law, or debated. Scripture is not a person and has no dates here. Topics with no recorded position say so and do not guess. Where Christians disagree, the views sit side by side. Read the seat's rule: "Scripture says X (Book c:v); applied here, Y." Never "God says".

## 1. Pricing and charging fairly
- **Stance:** Honest weights and measures are required. Sell at a fair price and do not wrong the other side of a deal. Applied here: the price the guest sees is the price you honestly stand behind.
- **Quote:** Proverbs 11:1: "A false balance is an abomination to Yahweh, but accurate weights are his delight." (S020)
- **Also:** Leviticus 25:14: "If you sell anything to your neighbor, or buy from your neighbor, you shall not wrong one another." (S003)
- **Context:** Proverb (description) plus Israel's law (Leviticus). The principle is fairness; no price level is set.

## 2. Two sets of rules for buyer and seller
- **Stance:** One standard for what you buy and another for what you sell is condemned. Applied here: do not hold guests to terms you would not accept from your landlord.
- **Quote:** Proverbs 20:10: "Differing weights and differing measures, both of them alike are an abomination to Yahweh." (S020)
- **Context:** Proverb. Repeated in Deuteronomy 25:13-16 (Israel's law, S005).

## 3. Hidden fees and honest listings
- **Stance:** Passing off lesser goods as full value, or hiding the catch, is called out by name. Applied here: cleaning fees, house rules, and photos should match what the guest gets. Inferred from S030, S023, S005.
- **Quote:** Amos 8:6: "and sell the sweepings with the wheat" (S030)
- **Also:** Isaiah 1:22: "Your silver has become dross, your wine mixed with water." (S023)
- **Context:** Prophetic charge against merchants in Israel (description of sin, not a rule list). Deuteronomy 25:14 is Israel's law on honest measures.

## 4. Deceptive deals and exaggerated claims
- **Stance:** Boasting of a gift or bargain that is not real is empty. Applied here: "was $400, now $250" with no real prior price fits this warning. Inferred from S020.
- **Quote:** Proverbs 25:14: "As clouds and wind without rain, so is he who boasts of gifts deceptively." (S020)
- **Context:** Proverb (description). It speaks to false gift-boasting; the fit to marketing is by application.

## 5. Guest reviews and honesty
- **Stance:** Do not give or bend testimony. Honest answers are prized, and truth is owed even to people who cannot check you. Applied here: no fake reviews, no review swaps, no pressure to change a review.
- **Quote:** Exodus 20:16: "You shall not give false testimony against your neighbor." (S002)
- **Also:** Proverbs 24:26: "An honest answer is like a kiss on the lips." (S020)
- **Context:** Command (Ten Commandments, Israel's law, affirmed in Romans 13:9 for believers). Reviews are not named; the principle is applied.

## 6. Guests who run down a listing to get a discount
- **Stance:** Scripture notes the haggler who disparages, then boasts. It records the habit without rule. Applied here: expect it, and answer with a fair, steady price.
- **Quote:** Proverbs 20:14: "but when he is gone his way, then he boasts." (S020)
- **Context:** Proverb (description). No command attached.

## 7. Discounting and generosity with what you own
- **Stance:** No recorded position on marketing discounts as such. The nearest text: an owner may be generous with his own money, and a seller must not wrong the buyer. Applied here: a discount is yours to give if it is honest and not a trick.
- **Quote:** Matthew 20:15: "Isn’t it lawful for me to do what I want to with what I own?" (S040)
- **Context:** Parable line about God's generosity; its single point is not a wage or pricing policy. Do not stretch it.

## 8. Debt, borrowing, and being owned by a lender
- **Stance:** Debt is treated as a form of power over the borrower. It is not banned. Pay what you borrow.
- **Quote:** Proverbs 22:7: "The rich rule over the poor. The borrower is servant to the lender." (S020)
- **Also:** Psalms 37:21: "The wicked borrow, and don’t pay back, but the righteous give generously." (S019)
- **Context:** Proverb and psalm (description). The warning is about repayment and power, not a flat ban.
- **Views side by side:** (a) Romans 13:8: "Owe no one anything, except to love one another" (S045) is read by some as a call to avoid debt altogether. (b) Others read it as: pay what is due when it is due, since the verse sits in a passage on paying taxes and owed respect (Romans 13:7). (c) Deuteronomy 15:6: "You will lend to many nations, but you will not borrow." (S005) is Israel's promised blessing, not a command to individuals today. Debated.

## 9. Personal guarantees and co-signing
- **Stance:** Guaranteeing another's debt is called a trap and a loss. Applied here: signing a personal guarantee on a lease or loan is the shape this warns against, so count the exit before you sign.
- **Quote:** Proverbs 11:15: "He who is collateral for a stranger will suffer for it, but he who refuses pledges of collateral is secure." (S020)
- **Also:** Proverbs 22:27: "If you don’t have means to pay, why should he take away your bed from under you?" (S020)
- **Context:** Proverb (description) with a father's warning; Proverbs 6:1-5 adds a way out. Paul voluntarily pledges for Onesimus in Philemon 1:18 (S057), so a pledge can be an act of love when chosen freely. Debated by application.

## 10. Leasing from a landlord and keeping agreements
- **Stance:** Keep your word even when it costs you. A signed lease binds you. A leased thing is held for the lease, and a borrower who damages something makes it good.
- **Quote:** Psalms 15:4: "he who keeps an oath even when it hurts, and doesn’t change" (S019)
- **Also:** Exodus 22:15: "If it is a leased thing, it came for its lease." (S002)
- **Context:** Psalm (description of the upright). Exodus is Israel's law on borrowed and hired animals; the lease principle is applied by analogy.

## 11. Tenants, guests, and treating them unfairly
- **Stance:** Do not drive people from their homes or rule over them with harshness. Foreigners and strangers are named for protection. Applied here: no retaliation, no withheld deposits, no cold treatment of a guest in trouble.
- **Quote:** Leviticus 19:33: "If a stranger lives as a foreigner with you in your land, you shall not do him wrong." (S003)
- **Also:** Micah 2:9: "You drive the women of my people out from their pleasant houses" (S033)
- **Context:** Israel's law (Leviticus) and a prophetic charge (Micah). Principle carried into hospitality in Hebrews 13:2.

## 12. Hospitality to strangers
- **Stance:** Welcoming strangers is ordinary Christian conduct, not an extra. Applied here: it fits a hosting business, and it also warns against hospitality that is only a transaction.
- **Quote:** Hebrews 13:2: "Don’t forget to show hospitality to strangers" (S058)
- **Also:** 1 Peter 4:9: "Be hospitable to one another without grumbling." (S060)
- **Context:** Command to believers. Written about welcoming people into homes, not paid lodging; the commercial application is inferred from S058, S060, S003.

## 13. Pay cleaners and workers on time
- **Stance:** Wages are paid the day they are earned. Withholding wages is called a cry that reaches God. This is the clearest, most repeated rule for this owner.
- **Quote:** Deuteronomy 24:15: "In his day you shall give him his wages, neither shall the sun go down on it" (S005)
- **Also:** James 5:4: "the wages of the laborers who mowed your fields, which you have kept back by fraud, cry out" (S059)
- **Also:** Leviticus 19:13: "The wages of a hired servant shall not remain with you all night until the morning." (S003)
- **Context:** Command (Israel's law, Deuteronomy and Leviticus) restated to the rich in James. A weekly payroll cycle is not specified; the principle is no stalling.

## 14. Treating workers justly
- **Stance:** Masters owe what is just and equal, and are told to give up threats. A faithful worker is worth his pay.
- **Quote:** Colossians 4:1: "Masters, give to your servants that which is just and equal, knowing that you also have a Master in heaven." (S051)
- **Also:** Luke 10:7: "for the laborer is worthy of his wages." (S042)
- **Context:** Command to masters in the household of Paul's day. The slave and servant setting does not match a modern contractor; the principle (justice, no threats) is what carries over.

## 15. Workers owe honest work
- **Stance:** Workers are also told to work with honesty and wholeheartedly. Applied here: you may expect real work from your help; it is not unjust.
- **Quote:** Colossians 3:23: "And whatever you do, work heartily, as for the Lord, and not for men" (S051)
- **Also:** Titus 2:10: "not stealing, but showing all good fidelity" (S056)
- **Context:** Command to servants (description of Paul's instruction). Not a license to demand excess; Colossians 4:1 binds the employer too.

## 16. Hiring the first helper
- **Stance:** Choose men who are able and honest, and test them before you hand over a role. Spread the load rather than carry it all. Applied here: test a cleaner or co-host on small jobs first.
- **Quote:** Exodus 18:21: "able men which fear God: men of truth, hating unjust gain" (S002)
- **Also:** 1 Timothy 3:10: "Let them also first be tested; then let them serve if they are blameless." (S054)
- **Also:** Proverbs 26:10: "As an archer who wounds all, so is he who hires a fool or he who hires those who pass by." (S020)
- **Context:** Description of Jethro's advice and Paul's rule for church roles. They are not employment rules; the fit is by application.

## 17. Firing and dismissal
- **Stance:** No recorded position on firing an employee as such. Scripture covers how to correct (privately first, Matthew 18:15), not how to terminate. Titus 3:10 is church discipline, not employment. Do not use it to justify a firing.
- **Quote:** Matthew 18:15: "go, show him his fault between you and him alone." (S040)
- **Context:** Command on a brother who sins. Applied here: correct privately first, and pay what is owed on the way out (see topic 13). Inferred from S040, S005.

## 18. Growth and ambition
- **Stance:** Honest work and faithful use of what you are given are praised. Eager hunger for riches and swallowing up house after house is warned against. Applied here: scale if it stays honest and you stay faithful at each size.
- **Quote:** Matthew 25:21: "You have been faithful over a few things, I will set you over many things." (S040)
- **Also:** Proverbs 28:20: "A faithful man is rich with blessings; but one who is eager to be rich will not go unpunished." (S020)
- **Also:** Isaiah 5:8: "Woe to those who join house to house, who lay field to field, until there is no room" (S023)
- **Context:** Parable line (point is faithfulness with entrusted things, not a growth plan), proverb, and a prophetic charge against land-grabbers. Mixed; read in tension.

## 19. Diversification
- **Stance:** One line speaks to spreading what you have. It is usually read as giving, and also read as spreading risk. Debated.
- **Quote:** Ecclesiastes 11:2: "Give a portion to seven, yes, even to eight; for you don’t know what evil will be on the earth." (S021)
- **Context:** Wisdom saying. Debated: some read it as generosity, some as spreading risk. Applied to a multi-property plan only by inference from S021.

## 20. Saving and planning
- **Stance:** Plan, store ahead, and know the state of your own affairs. Hold the plan loosely, since the outcome is not yours to control.
- **Quote:** Proverbs 21:5: "The plans of the diligent surely lead to profit; and everyone who is hasty surely rushes to poverty." (S020)
- **Also:** Genesis 41:35: "Let them gather all the food of these good years that come, and store grain under the hand of Pharaoh for food in the cities, and let them keep it." (S001)
- **Also:** Proverbs 27:23: "Know well the state of your flocks, and pay attention to your herds" (S020)
- **Also:** Proverbs 16:9: "A man’s heart plans his course, but Yahweh directs his steps." (S020)
- **Context:** Proverbs (description) and Joseph's plan in Egypt (narrative). Luke 12:20 (S042, rich fool) warns against saving that ignores God.

## 21. Counting the cost
- **Stance:** Count the cost before starting. Applied here: before a new lease, run the numbers on what happens if occupancy drops.
- **Quote:** Luke 14:28: "For which of you, desiring to build a tower, doesn’t first sit down and count the cost, to see if he has enough to complete it?" (S042)
- **Context:** Teaching on discipleship; the point is not business planning, but the principle is stated plainly.

## 22. Taxes and authorities
- **Stance:** Pay what you owe. Rulers are called servants for order, and taxes are named. Scripture also sets a limit: obey God over men when they conflict.
- **Quote:** Romans 13:7: "if you owe taxes, pay taxes; if customs, then customs" (S045)
- **Also:** Mark 12:17: "Render to Caesar the things that are Caesar’s, and to God the things that are God’s." (S041)
- **Also:** Acts 5:29: "We must obey God rather than men." (S044)
- **Context:** Command (Romans) and Jesus's answer to a trap question (Mark). Acts 5:29 is Peter before a ruling council on preaching, not a tax rule. Christians debate how far the limit reaches.

## 23. Tax avoidance versus evasion
- **Stance:** No recorded position on legal tax planning (avoidance) as a category. Scripture speaks to paying what you owe and to not collecting more than is due. Evasion that hides income is covered by the honesty and truthfulness texts. Do not read an endorsement of tax strategy into silence.
- **Quote:** Luke 3:13: "Collect no more than that which is appointed to you." (S042)
- **Also:** Romans 13:7: "give everyone what you owe" (S045)
- **Context:** Command to tax collectors (Luke 3:13) and to believers (Romans 13:7). Inferred from S042, S045, S003 for the evasion line.

## 24. Restitution when you have wronged someone
- **Stance:** If you have taken more than you should, return it with interest. Applied here: if a fee overcharged a guest, refund and add something.
- **Quote:** Luke 19:8: "If I have wrongfully exacted anything of anyone, I restore four times as much." (S042)
- **Also:** Exodus 22:1: "he shall pay five oxen for an ox, and four sheep for a sheep." (S002)
- **Context:** Zacchaeus's own vow (description, not a command to others) and Israel's law on theft. The fourfold rate is not a standard for every case.

## 25. Giving and tithing
- **Stance:** Give first, give gladly, and do not hold back what you owe to the needy. The size is where Christians differ.
- **Quote:** Proverbs 3:9: "Honor Yahweh with your substance, with the first fruits of all your increase" (S020)
- **Also:** 2 Corinthians 9:7: "Let each man give according as he has determined in his heart, not grudgingly or under compulsion, for God loves a cheerful giver." (S047)
- **Views side by side:** (a) The tithe still stands as the standard. Supporting texts: Malachi 3:10: "Bring the whole tithe into the storehouse, that there may be food in my house, and test me now in this" (S039), and Deuteronomy 14:22: "You shall surely tithe all the increase of your seed" (S005). (b) The tithe was Israel's law; the New Testament gives no fixed percentage but sets freedom, planning and gladness (2 Corinthians 9:7; 1 Corinthians 16:2: "let each one of you save, as he may prosper" (S046)). Debated. Do not pick one for the owner.

## 26. Giving to the poor and the needy
- **Stance:** Open-handedness is repeated across both Testaments, and closing your eyes has a cost.
- **Quote:** Proverbs 28:27: "One who gives to the poor has no lack; but one who closes his eyes will have many curses." (S020)
- **Also:** Acts 20:35: "so laboring you ought to help the weak, and to remember the words of the Lord Jesus, that he himself said, ‘It is more blessed to give than to receive.’" (S044)
- **Context:** Proverb and Paul's farewell. Descriptions of a pattern, not guarantees of repayment.

## 27. Work hours and rest
- **Stance:** Six days of work, one of rest, for servants and strangers too. Applied here: your cleaners and helpers need rest as well as you.
- **Quote:** Exodus 23:12: "Six days you shall do your work, and on the seventh day you shall rest, that your ox and your donkey may have rest, and the son of your servant, and the alien may be refreshed." (S002)
- **Views side by side:** (a) The Sabbath rhythm still binds as a principle of weekly rest. (b) Colossians 2:16: "Let no one therefore judge you in eating, or in drinking, or with respect to a feast day or a new moon or a Sabbath day" (S051) is read as freeing believers from a fixed day. Debated.
- **Context:** Israel's law (Exodus), with a New Testament text that tells believers not to judge each other over days.

## 28. Burnout and overwork
- **Stance:** Toil without rest is vanity, and sleep is a gift. A leader who carries it all wears out. Applied here: delegate, and do not run on anxiety.
- **Quote:** Psalms 127:2: "It is vain for you to rise up early, to stay up late, eating the bread of toil, for he gives sleep to his loved ones." (S019)
- **Also:** Exodus 18:18: "You will surely wear away, both you, and this people that is with you; for the thing is too heavy for you." (S002)
- **Also:** Ecclesiastes 4:6: "Better is a handful, with quietness, than two handfuls with labor and chasing after wind." (S021)
- **Context:** Psalm and wisdom literature (description) plus Jethro's counsel to Moses (narrative). Diligence is also praised (Proverbs 10:4, S020); hold both.

## 29. Anxiety about money and the future
- **Stance:** Do not be anxious for tomorrow. Put worries on God, while still doing the work.
- **Quote:** Matthew 6:34: "don’t be anxious for tomorrow, for tomorrow will be anxious for itself." (S040)
- **Also:** 1 Peter 5:7: "casting all your worries on him, because he cares for you." (S060)
- **Context:** Command from the Sermon on the Mount and from Peter. They do not cancel planning (see topic 20); they target worry.

## 30. Contentment and the love of money
- **Stance:** Enough is a goal. The love of money, not money itself, is the root named.
- **Quote:** 1 Timothy 6:6: "But godliness with contentment is great gain." (S054)
- **Also:** 1 Timothy 6:10: "For the love of money is a root of all kinds of evil." (S054)
- **Also:** Hebrews 13:5: "Be free from the love of money, content with such things as you have" (S058)
- **Context:** Command and teaching to believers. Wealth itself is not condemned: Ecclesiastes 5:19 (S021) calls the power to enjoy it a gift.

## 31. Competition and envy
- **Stance:** Do not envy a rival's success or let comparison drive you. Examine your own work.
- **Quote:** Galatians 6:4: "But let each man examine his own work, and then he will have reason to boast in himself, and not in someone else." (S048)
- **Also:** Ecclesiastes 4:4: "Then I saw all the labor and achievement that is the envy of a man’s neighbor." (S021)
- **Context:** Command (Galatians) and observation (Ecclesiastes). No recorded position on pricing against competitors or market strategy.

## 32. Speech, promises and contracts
- **Stance:** Let your yes be yes. Pay your vows. Better not to promise than to promise and fail.
- **Quote:** James 5:12: "let your “yes” be “yes”, and your “no”, “no”, so that you don’t fall into hypocrisy." (S059)
- **Also:** Ecclesiastes 5:5: "It is better that you should not vow, than that you should vow and not pay." (S021)
- **Context:** Command (James, restating Jesus in Matthew 5:37, S040). Applies to agreements, not only oaths.

## 33. Lawsuits and disputes
- **Stance:** Settle quickly and prefer to be wronged over fighting believers in court. Go to the person first. Applied here: with a landlord or a fellow church member, try private talks and a mediator before any suit.
- **Quote:** 1 Corinthians 6:7: "Why not rather be wronged? Why not rather be defrauded?" (S046)
- **Also:** Matthew 5:25: "Agree with your adversary quickly, while you are with him on the way" (S040)
- **Also:** Proverbs 25:8: "Don’t be hasty in bringing charges to court." (S020)
- **Context:** Command about disputes between believers (1 Corinthians) and Jesus's teaching. Debated whether this bars all suits against non-believers; the text addresses disputes inside the church.

## 34. Counsel and advisors
- **Stance:** Plans fail without counsel. Listen, and weigh who is advising you. Rehoboam shows the cost of dropping good counsel.
- **Quote:** Proverbs 15:22: "Where there is no counsel, plans fail; but in a multitude of counselors they are established." (S020)
- **Also:** Proverbs 12:15: "The way of a fool is right in his own eyes, but he who is wise listens to counsel." (S020)
- **Context:** Proverbs (description). Not every adviser is wise; 1 Kings 12:8 (S011) records Rehoboam dropping the counsel of the old men.

## 35. Raising money, partners and equity
- **Stance:** No recorded position on investors, equity splits or venture capital. Related texts: two together do better than one, and promises to partners are binding. Do not treat these as advice on terms.
- **Quote:** Ecclesiastes 4:9: "Two are better than one, because they have a good reward for their labor." (S021)
- **Also:** Amos 3:3: "Do two walk together, unless they have agreed?" (S030)
- **Context:** Observation (Ecclesiastes) and a rhetorical question in a prophet's argument (Amos), not about business partners. See also topic 9 on guarantees and topic 32 on promises.

## 36. Using AI, automation and leverage
- **Stance:** No recorded position. Scripture does not address AI or automation. The nearest general principles are faithful use of what you are given, honesty in what a tool says on your behalf, and not trusting tools over people you serve.
- **Quote:** Luke 16:10: "He who is faithful in a very little is faithful also in much. He who is dishonest in a very little is also dishonest in much." (S042)
- **Context:** Teaching on faithfulness. Inferred only; it is not a statement about technology.

## 37. Selling the business
- **Stance:** No recorded position on selling a company. The Jubilee rule in Leviticus 25 sets land sales by crop-years, which is Israel's law and not a method for pricing a business. The honest-dealing rule still applies to the buyer.
- **Quote:** Leviticus 25:16: "for he is selling the number of the crops to you." (S003)
- **Also:** Leviticus 25:14: "If you sell anything to your neighbor, or buy from your neighbor, you shall not wrong one another." (S003)
- **Context:** Israel's law on land. Applied here: tell the buyer the truth about the lease terms, ratings and costs.
