# Stories: Tom Wheelwright

20 stories and cases he reuses, with the lesson he draws. Source ids in parentheses. Most-used first. Retellings are paraphrase; only the quoted words are his.

1. **The pharmacy owners and the $400,000 bill.** Two pharmacy owners were told by their accountant they would owe $400,000 and might close. They pressed him on the inventory deduction Tom had mentioned in a talk. The CPA agreed, and the tax fell to $45,000. Lesson: ask the question, press your CPA, and pick one who is willing to learn (S011, S050).
2. **The first audit he lost on purpose.** A year out of grad school, on a derivatives deal, he ran the numbers and the client was better off losing. Lesson: "sometimes you win when you lose." (S010). A second client audit years later went the same way (S010).
3. **Twelve inches of documentation.** A client with four three-inch binders had a clean audit that ended three days after the supervisor meeting. His wife Luanne's client sent binders too, and the auditor stopped. Lesson: documentation ends audits (S008, S010).
4. **The audit with no records.** A young woman was audited with no documents. He could not rebuild them two or three years on, and with contemporaneous records he thinks they would have won 90%. Lesson: record at the time of the expense (S008).
5. **The Kiyosaki partnership and the Chile car deduction.** Kiyosaki challenged him on stage to show how to deduct a car. Tom found a way and changed his view from "most things are deductible" to "ask how". Lesson: change "can I?" into "how can I?" (S037). Also: in 2003 Kiyosaki pulled him on stage on depreciation, which became the "it's magic" answer (S044). On a plane in Eastern Europe he gave Kiyosaki an idea that saved hundreds of thousands of dollars (S037). He travels with Kiyosaki, and the question that comes up each time is where to put your money (S005).
6. **The IRS lien by mistake.** An IRS error put a lien on his company's payroll account and he never saw the notice. It took years (S022) or eight years (S028) to remove. The two sources differ on the time. Lesson: never ignore notices, and avoid liens at almost any cost (S022, S028).
7. **Lost a lawsuit he thought was iron clad.** A former partner sued. The mediator put his odds at 80 to 90%. It cost about half a million dollars, versus a $100,000 early settlement. Lesson: courts are unpredictable, and "the true winners are the attorneys." (S009). The first lawsuit, by contrast, was handled and paid for by the insurer's lawyers (S009, S044).
8. **The funded and unfunded trusts.** A favorite client had a plan but never moved title. He died, and his girlfriend, two ex-wives and three children took two to three years and hundreds of thousands in fees (S008, S027). A doctor friend funded his trust, and the widow was told there was nothing left to do (S009, S027). Lesson: execute the plan, retitle the assets.
9. **JP Newman and the carried-interest change.** A client under contract on a 1,062-unit sale saw the law change on December 23. Tom pushed a 1031 into a 200-unit Austin building. JP resisted, then found a good asset. Lesson: the law showed the way, but never let the tax tail wag the dog (S003).
10. **Price Waterhouse fired him after seven months.** They had recruited him to run multi-state tax in Phoenix, with two young boys at home. He chose entrepreneurs and started his own firm. Lesson: a paycheck is not security, and the wrong seat is not the wrong skill (S006, S026, S052).
11. **One client versus two hundred.** As an in-house tax lead at a Fortune 500 company his first task was cutting half the department. Lesson: an employee has one client and a business owner has many, so one loss is survivable (S044, S045, S046).
12. **The nurse who lost on 2,300 hours.** A full-time nurse logged 2,300 real estate hours against 2,200 as a nurse but had 25 properties and could not show 500 hours on each. Lesson: make the aggregation election and count hours by activity (S044).
13. **Hiring his own children and the family business.** He grew up working in his father's printing plant, which taught him to work (S040, S012). He tells clients to pay kids for real work. He also saw kids' marriages break up over an income-splitting plan, and a daughter whose husband learned of her trust from a joint return (S016). Lesson: real work, documented, and open family talk first.
14. **The syndicator who took $45,000 instead of $1.1 million.** A syndicator skipped bonus depreciation because he had it backwards. Lesson: know the rules, and make your CPA know them (S050).
15. **The Dallas Amazon sellers.** He announced an inventory deduction to applause. A CPA said it would draw a 40% penalty. He spent about four hours in the Bluebook and found a footnote. "Could I have been wrong? I could’ve." (S050). Lesson: question advisors, read the source, stay open to being wrong.
16. **The captive that charged $40,000.** A client's captive priced IRS-audit-fee risk at $40,000 a year. Tom offered to cover audits for $10,000 less. Lesson: "That is just not a realistic premium." (S015). A big tax benefit demands a real purpose and perfect paperwork.
17. **The 2008 appreciation loss.** He bought in Phoenix in 2003 during the boom and "lost money in that 2008 from relying too much on appreciation" (S013). He also bought in 2003 to 2006 and did not sell (S047). Lesson: cash flow is the buffer, and he is a much bigger investor in business than in real estate (S013, S047).
18. **The insurer's attorney in his Utah rental.** A renter claimed a slip and fall after losing a job. Five minutes on the phone with the insurance adjuster was the end of it for him. Lesson: insurance gets you the attorney, which beats the LLC alone (S044, S009).
19. **Two bears and a guard dog.** You do not have to outrun the bear, only your buddy. His big dog and alarms make the neighbor the easier target. Lesson: aim to be the hardest target, not to win (S009, S044).
20. **The client he fired for fake inventory.** A client would not take a physical inventory at year end, and the numbers could not be true. He dropped them. Lesson: honest books are a condition of working with him (S024).
