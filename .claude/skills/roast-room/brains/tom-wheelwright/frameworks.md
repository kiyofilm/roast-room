# Frameworks: Tom Wheelwright

Built from 55 sources (podcast episodes 2018 to 2024, two blog posts, one BiggerPockets interview), read through five batches of verified notes. 25 frameworks, most-used first. "Sources" counts distinct sources that use the idea (ids listed). Names are his where he named them; otherwise a short name marked *(unnamed)*. "How to score" lines are scoring guidance for the council built from his stated thresholds, not quotes.

**Tax law has changed since these sources (2018 to 2024).** Every law claim below carries its source date ("as of ..."). Treat rates, thresholds, bonus percentages and deadlines as stale until a licensed advisor confirms them. **Gap:** no source covers a leased-home arbitrage business (tenant who leases, furnishes and rents nightly). No source states a short-term-rental hour test beyond one remark in S044 (see 17). Where that matters, this file says "no recorded position".

## 1. The tax law is a set of incentives
- **What it is:** Almost all of the code pays people to do what governments want: create jobs (business), housing, energy, food, give to charity. The question is never "how do I dodge" but "what does the law reward, and can I do it."
- **Parts:** Name what the government wants; do it; take the incentive. Business owners and investors get the most. The same structure holds in every developed country (S021, S029, S037, S044).
- **When to use:** First question on any money decision, and the right reframe when a founder says taxes are a punishment.
- **How to score:** 5 if the plan is the activity the law explicitly rewards (employ people, own housing, produce energy); 3 if it is reasonable but only incidentally rewarded; 1 if it exists only to produce a deduction.
- **Quote:** "Remember, the tax law is a series of incentives." (S055)
- **Quote:** "the biggest activity the tax law wants to incentivize is business" (S021)
- **Sources:** 20 (S001, S002, S004, S013, S015, S020, S021, S022, S024, S028, S029, S030, S032, S033, S037, S042, S043, S044, S045, S055)
- **Dated:** as of 2018-04-13 he says most of the code is incentives (S001). He says incentives shift but do not go away (S032, 2020-12-10; S055, 2020-12-10). That is his prediction, not law.

## 2. Change your facts
- **What it is:** Your tax result follows your facts: what you earn, how you earn it, which entity, what you spend. A CPA cannot save you tax; only changed actions can.
- **Parts:** Learn the options; pick the facts to change; act, then document; report correctly.
- **When to use:** Whenever a founder asks for a "tax strategy" but has not decided to change anything.
- **How to score:** 5 if a named action with a date and an owner exists; 3 if the plan is agreed but not started; 1 if the founder is waiting for the accountant to fix it.
- **Quote:** "If you want to change your tax, you have to change your facts." (S008)
- **Quote:** "your CPA, your tax advisor cannot save you a dime. It's your actions that create tax savings." (S008)
- **Sources:** 11 (S008, S012, S019, S023, S024, S028, S038, S042, S044, S053, S055)

## 3. Professional versus amateur investor: one decision, repeated
- **What it is:** The amateur decides fresh every time. The professional sets criteria once (type, size, place, loan) and applies them over and over. Also: pick one asset class, and fit the class to the person's natural instinct (he uses the Kolbe assessment).
- **Parts:** One asset class; fixed criteria; copy a proven investor's criteria ("R&D, rip off and duplicate"); match the work to the person; add a team strong where you are weak. Retail, wholesale, direct ladder (S048): each step up adds control, tax benefit and return.
- **When to use:** Founder is scattered across ideas, or considering a second line of business before the first is systemized.
- **How to score:** 5 if the founder can state the buy box in one sentence and has repeated it; 3 if the criteria exist but get broken; 1 if each deal is a fresh decision.
- **Quote:** "An amateur investor is somebody who makes a new decision every time there's an investment opportunity." (S005)
- **Quote:** "pick that niche, get really good at it and just make a decision, the same decision. And just do it over and over and over again." (S034)
- **Sources:** 8 (S005, S006, S030, S031, S034, S044, S047, S048)
- **Dated:** the return ranges for retail, wholesale and direct (about 5 to 10%, 10 to 20%, 20 to 40%) are teaching estimates, as of 2018-12-20 (S048), not data.

## 4. The five ways to save tax
- **What it is:** (1) deductions, (2) conversion (change high-taxed income into low-taxed), (3) other people's brackets (kids, parents, entities), (4) credits and non-taxable income, (5) deferral. Deferral is last and least.
- **Parts:** Take permanent savings before temporary ones. In S012 he words them as deduction, conversion, elimination, deferral. S015 names conversion and deferral.
- **When to use:** Checklist when a founder asks "what can I do."
- **How to score:** Count how many of the first four are used before deferral is considered. All four used, 5; two, 3; only deferral or only deductions, 1.
- **Quote:** "Once we get it, we never have to give it back." (S002)
- **Sources:** 5 (S002, S008, S012, S015, S055)
- **Dated:** as of 2018-05-02 ordinary income taxed up to 37%, capital gains and dividends up to 20% (S002).

## 5. Four tests for a deduction, and documentation
- **What it is:** A cost is deductible only with a business purpose, if it is ordinary, necessary, and documented. Nothing is deductible unless the law allows it.
- **Parts:** Purpose; ordinary; necessary; documentation at the time of the expense (receipts, not card statements); logs, minutes, loan papers. S002 lists three tests, S021, S040 and S042 add documentation as the fourth.
- **When to use:** Any expense, trip, family payment, home office, or loan from the business.
- **How to score:** 5 if all four are met with contemporaneous records; 3 if purpose is clear but records are rebuilt later; 1 if records are missing (he says no documentation, no deduction).
- **Quote:** "If it's not documented, it's not a deduction." (S021)
- **Quote:** "If you don't have the documentation, you don't get the deduction." (S008)
- **Sources:** 6 (S002, S008, S010, S021, S040, S042)

## 6. Become a business first, in an entity, never a Schedule C
- **What it is:** The law rewards owning a business, so employees and sole proprietors should become a real business with an entity, payroll, books and a return. A Schedule C shows only a profit and loss, so he says it draws more audits and fewer benefits.
- **Parts:** Become a business; form an entity; choose the tax election (S corporation if you take the money out, C corporation if you reinvest; partnership for distributions only); pay a reasonable salary through a payroll company; deduct what passes the four tests. Rental real estate is a separate discussion (S021).
- **When to use:** Any founder still running on a sole proprietor or personal-name setup, or planning to hire.
- **How to score:** 5 if entity, bank account, payroll and books exist and the election was chosen on purpose; 3 if an entity exists but the election was never reviewed; 1 if sole proprietor.
- **Quote:** "If we're going to get the tax benefits of the big guys, we have to behave like the big guys" (S021)
- **Quote:** "S corporation, one level of tax. Only the income tax, no tax on the distribution." (S023)
- **Sources:** 8 (S002, S019, S020, S021, S023, S042, S044, S053)
- **Dated:** as of 2019-09-13 C corporation rate 21%, down from 35% (S021). As of 2019-08-15 he puts the top effective individual rate near 29.6% after the 20% deduction, and a distributed C corp profit can bring the combined rate back near 37% (S019). Tension on salary rules in the S corporation: see principles.md, "Tensions".

## 7. How to test and choose an advisor
- **What it is:** Your advisor is the most important person in your financial life after your spouse, so interview them and keep interviewing. A good one plans forward, is not afraid of the IRS, handles IRS contact, meets you at least quarterly, and will learn.
- **Parts:** Is the advisor proactive or a historian; do they ask what you want; do they say "how" or just "no"; do they handle audits and show examples; do they skip legitimate deductions from fear; will they read his book; are they the same person as the preparer; do they refer out specialist work.
- **When to use:** Any time a founder reports "my CPA says you can't."
- **How to score:** Five yes answers on proactive, unafraid, meets quarterly, explains in plain English, brings specialists: 5. Two or fewer: change advisor.
- **Quote:** "the single most important person in your financial life outside of your spouse, is your tax advisor." (S037)
- **Quote:** "If your tax preparer says you can't, the question should be, what are they missing?" (S011)
- **Sources:** 12 (S006, S010, S011, S015, S019, S020, S024, S037, S043, S050, S052, S053)
- **Bias flag:** he promotes the WealthAbility Network of vetted advisors as the way to find one (S043, 2020-12-04). Weigh that as marketing.

## 8. Hire your children (and parents)
- **What it is:** Pay children for real work in your business: a deduction to you, little or no tax to them, and the money can be held in a trust you control. Parents can own part of a business; children can too but the kiddie tax makes it harder (S040).
- **Parts:** Real job; reasonable pay; W-2 preferred (S012); hours and pay documented; bank account; a trust or custodial account to control the money; a team to set it up.
- **When to use:** Founder wants to pay family. A real fit for a small business hiring help.
- **How to score:** 5 if the work is real, the pay is market, records exist, and family can discuss money openly; 1 if the pay is for photos, nominal work, or the family cannot talk about money (he refuses to use the plan in that case, S016).
- **Quote:** "It has to be reasonable. You can't go paying a baby $12,000 to take a couple of pictures of them." (S040)
- **Quote:** "I've actually seen kids' marriages break up over this income tax plan." (S016)
- **Sources:** 8 (S002, S012, S016, S040, S042, S044, S045, S053)
- **Dated:** the $12,000 figure is as of 2018-05-02 (S002), 2018-08-01 (S040), 2019-12-20 (S053) and 2020-11-30 (S042); he ties it to the child's standard deduction. Check the current amount and payroll tax rules for minors. As of 2018-05-02 he says no Social Security tax under age 18 when the parent's own company pays (S002); S040 says the payroll tax and entity details are not covered.

## 9. Know your numbers: records, then analysis, then decision
- **What it is:** Good decisions need good information. Hire a bookkeeper who is not a relative, set a simple chart of accounts, review with the CPA, and watch a few numbers on a fixed cadence.
- **Parts:** Five steps to accurate data (S014); cadence of one number daily (cash), two weekly, three monthly; books weekly; CPA review monthly or at least quarterly; three statements; purpose of each item (an expense exists to create income, an asset creates income or cuts an expense, a liability buys an asset); compare actual to expected and sell what no longer meets criteria.
- **When to use:** Founder cannot say what the property or business earned last month.
- **How to score:** 5 if cash is checked daily, books close weekly and the CPA reviews at least quarterly; 3 if books are monthly; 1 if records are rebuilt at tax time.
- **Quote:** "it's worse to have bad data than no data" (S014)
- **Quote:** "We make good decisions when we have good information." (S007)
- **Sources:** 8 (S007, S012, S014, S021, S023, S042, S044, S051)

## 10. Depreciation, bonus depreciation and cost segregation
- **What it is:** Depreciation lets you deduct an asset that is rising in value. A purchase has parts: land (not depreciable), building, and shorter-life items such as contents and land improvements. Cost segregation moves price into the fast bucket and bonus depreciation lets you deduct it now.
- **Parts:** Run the numbers before ordering a study; buy-and-hold beats flip because a flip is a business at ordinary rates (S044); recapture is overblown because it turns ordinary income into capital gain (S044); the benefit does not move the risk to the government (S013).
- **When to use:** Any purchase of equipment, furnishings or property.
- **How to score:** Guidance from his examples only: he expects roughly 20 to 30% of a purchase price in the fast bucket (S036 says 30%, S044 says 20 to 30%, S055 says 20 to 30%); a $1,000,000 building can give a $200,000 to $300,000 first-year deduction (S044). If a study costs more than the tax value of the fast bucket, skip it.
- **Quote:** "I call this the magic is depreciation" (S029)
- **Quote:** "I've seen cost segregations for small properties be as little as $2,000 and say $15,000" (S044)
- **Sources:** 11 (S011, S013, S018, S029, S030, S035, S036, S042, S044, S050, S055)
- **Dated (the percentages moved):** as of 2018-08-20 about 30% of a purchase deductible (S036); as of 2019-03-06 a $100,000 investment could produce up to $150,000 of deduction, his claim as heard (S011); as of 2022-02-10 100% bonus on land improvements and contents "for the next couple of years in a decreasing amount" (S044); as of 2024-01-11 80% for the year and 60% the next, with a pending bill to restore 100% that was a proposal, not law (S035). S050 (2019-05-21) says you can only take bonus on the equipment portion by doing a cost segregation. All four are stale: check the current schedule.
- **Gap:** all of this assumes the investor owns the building. S036 does not address a leased property. No recorded position on a tenant who owns only furniture.

## 11. Partner with the government, and it shares your risk
- **What it is:** You are a partner with the government whether you want to be or not. Either a silent partner who lets them take what they take, or an active partner who does what they want and takes the incentive. When you take risk in a business or investment, the government shares it through deductions.
- **Parts:** Employees and most small owners are silent partners; big business owners and professional investors are active partners (S044). Reinvesting the saved tax compounds it (S001).
- **When to use:** Founder thinks of tax as a cost to minimize rather than a partner to engage.
- **How to score:** Judge each dollar of planned spend by whether the government shares it (deduction, credit, depreciation). Spending with no sharing is a pure after-tax cost.
- **Quote:** "you are a partner with the government whether you like it or not." (S001)
- **Quote:** "If you take risk, the government will share that risk." (S029)
- **Sources:** 7 (S001, S015, S028, S029, S030, S044, S055)
- **Caution (his own):** a deduction at most returns 40 to 50% of the money, so you still pay the rest (S055, 2020-12-10).

## 12. Cash flow first, cap rate above interest rate, judge after tax
- **What it is:** Buy for cash flow. Tax benefits come second and appreciation third. Compare deals after tax. Cap rate must exceed the loan rate or leverage works against you.
- **Parts:** Five ways real estate pays (appreciation, cash flow, tenant loan paydown, inflation, tax), cash flow first (S013); good debt is fixed rate, amortizing, cash-flowing; no interest-only (S013); do not buy when cap rate is below the loan rate (S007, S035, S041); investment value is its cash flow (S030).
- **When to use:** Any lease, buy or expansion decision.
- **How to score:** 5 if after-tax cash on cash is positive and cap rate beats the rate by a clear margin; 3 if it works only with the tax benefit; 1 if it works only with appreciation.
- **Quote:** "Nobody cares about before-tax rate of return. It's all after-tax rate of return." (S034)
- **Quote:** "Your cap rate needs to be above your interest rate, otherwise you are losing money." (S007)
- **Sources:** 7 (S007, S013, S018, S030, S034, S035, S041)
- **Tension (his own order):** in S018 (2019-08-01) he says they never focus on tax benefits or capital gains, then ranks tax benefits second. He also admits he lost money in 2008 relying on appreciation (S013, S047).

## 13. Ask how, not whether
- **What it is:** When a deduction or structure seems unavailable, ask how to qualify. Preparers who say "you can't" are often afraid, or missing something.
- **Parts:** Reframe the question; press the advisor; verify; do not trust him blindly ("please don't trust me", S011).
- **When to use:** A founder reports a no from an advisor, or tries to decide without asking.
- **How to score:** 5 if the founder asked "how" and got a written path or a clear no with a reason; 1 if the founder accepted a bare no.
- **Quote:** "The question is not can you qualify. The question is how can you qualify?" (S011)
- **Quote:** "Instead of asking, is this market deductible? The better question is, how do I make it deductible?" (S044)
- **Sources:** 4 (S011, S019, S037, S044)
- **Slip flagged:** S037 records that he used to say "most things are deductible, some aren't" and now says everything is possibly deductible with the right facts, after a challenge from Kiyosaki. That is an aggressive stance; test with a licensed advisor.

## 14. Three rules to never fear an audit
- **What it is:** (1) never speak to the IRS yourself, your advisor does; (2) keep documentation; (3) have the preparer keep work papers that defend the return. Pay small true mistakes. File even when you cannot pay. Prepare every return as if it will be audited.
- **Parts:** Respect the auditor, limit the scope, run the numbers on losing an issue before fighting it (he once lost on purpose, "sometimes you win when you lose", S010), settle at the lowest level, avoid court, go to an attorney past a field audit (S039). Never lie to the IRS.
- **When to use:** Founder worries that aggressive planning invites an audit, or gets a notice.
- **How to score:** 5 if records are current, an advisor who handles the IRS is in place, and the return is prepared for audit; 1 if the founder would answer the IRS directly.
- **Quote:** "I will never speak to a government tax authority." (S010)
- **Quote:** "My job is to help the IRS auditor do their job." (S039)
- **Sources:** 6 (S008, S010, S020, S022, S039, S043)
- **Dated:** as of 2019-09-26 failure to file is 5% of unpaid tax a month up to 25%, failure to pay half a percent a month (S022); repeated as of 2020-07-13 (S028). As of 2019-03-06 you are not required to speak to the IRS unless a subpoena is issued (S010). As of 2023-06-23 he expects more audits from new IRS hires (S041).
- **Slip flagged:** he says he has been a CPA "almost 40 years" in S020 (2019-08-29) and "35 years" in S022 (2019-09-26). Do not state a number.

## 15. The architect and the builder: team and order of work
- **What it is:** The tax advisor designs the structure; the attorney reviews and builds it; the bookkeeper keeps it clean; the banker lends. Do not do it alone.
- **Parts:** Order he gives (S008): strategy, team, CPA draws the diagram, attorney sets up entities, bank account per entity, transfer title, documentation, credit cards by entity, estimated taxes, daily habits. The CPA and attorney must talk (S016). Right person for the right part (S009).
- **When to use:** Founder is about to set up entities or restructure.
- **How to score:** 5 if CPA and attorney have both signed off in sequence; 3 if only one has; 1 if self-directed.
- **Quote:** "The tax advisor is really the architect and the attorney is the builder." (S009)
- **Quote:** "Investing is a team sport." (S044)
- **Sources:** 7 (S008, S009, S016, S043, S044, S052, S053)
- **Limit:** he says he is not an attorney and defers to one on entity choice, state selection and asset protection (S009, S023, S044).

## 16. Never let the tax tail wag the dog
- **What it is:** Do the deal for sound business reasons; the tax result is a bonus. Tax benefit is a reason to look and study, never the reason to buy.
- **Parts:** Weigh tax saving against financial risk; do not pay a bigger loss to save tax; buy the asset you want for the long term; a deduction still costs most of the dollar.
- **When to use:** Any year-end purchase, vehicle, property or insurance idea pitched on the deduction.
- **How to score:** 5 if the founder would do it with no tax benefit; 3 if marginal; 1 if it only works after tax.
- **Quote:** "Don't ever do anything just for tax purposes, do it because there's sound business reasons to do it" (S055)
- **Quote:** "Let the tax advantages incentivize you to look and do your homework." (S013)
- **Sources:** 4 (S003, S013, S053, S055). Echoed by S015 (a captive is only for people who need the insurance).

## 17. Passive versus active, material participation, and the short-term-rental seven-day rule
- **What it is:** Rental real estate is passive by default, so its losses only offset passive income, unless you qualify as a real estate professional or find another route. He also flips it: make active income passive so rental losses offset it.
- **Parts:** Real estate professional status (one spouse is enough); material participation tested per activity at 500 hours, with an election to treat all rentals as one activity; a property with an average rental of seven days or less is a business, not a rental, so you test participation like any business (S044); do not combine short-term and long-term rentals when testing (S044); a property manager reduces your own hours (S044); a child's trust can hold part of a business so income is passive to the trust (S044, S016).
- **When to use:** An owner or operator wants to use depreciation losses against other income.
- **How to score:** No numeric score from him. Guidance: count your own hours per activity before outsourcing management.
- **Quote:** "If you have a property that rents for seven days or less, it's not under the tax law definition, it's not a rental, it's a business." (S044)
- **Quote:** "you can't combine your short-term rentals with your long-term rentals." (S044)
- **Sources:** 6 (S011, S016, S030, S034, S044, S048)
- **Dated and conflicting:** as of 2022-02-10 (S044) he gives the 500 hours test and says the transcript gives the real estate professional test as "150 hours" in one place and 750 hours later. The note flags it as a slip. The statute is more than 750 hours and more than half of personal services, per the note author, not Tom. As of 2023-01-06 (S034) he says STR losses need less time than long-term rental losses but never names an hours test or an average-stay test, and says "I am too" learning how to turn STRs into passive income. As of 2020-10-15 (S030) he says he can usually find a way for passive investors to use deductions up front but does not explain how.
- **No recorded position:** a leased-home arbitrage operator (not the owner, so no building depreciation), the tenant's own participation hours, the effect of hiring a cleaner or manager on those hours for an arbitrage business, hours counted for a spouse.

## 18. Buy, borrow, die
- **What it is:** Buy an appreciating asset with other people's money, take the depreciation, borrow against it instead of selling (loans are not income), exchange (1031) instead of sell, hold to death for a stepped-up basis. The deduction can fund the next down payment.
- **Parts:** "201" steps: buy with bank money and take bonus; 1031 to change property type; borrow, do not sell; hold. Deduction-to-down-payment loop: a $200,000 deduction at 40% is $80,000, and at 20% down buys about $400,000 more (S035). Tax is his "fourth inflation hedge" (S035).
- **When to use:** Founder asks what to do with cash or a refinance.
- **How to score:** Guidance only: works if cash flow covers the debt first (S044) and the founder will hold; fails if the asset is not owned (a lease).
- **Quote:** "the reason that you don't pay tax when you borrow is because you have to pay it back." (S036)
- **Quote:** "So I see tax as a fourth inflation hedge." (S035)
- **Sources:** 6 (S001, S013, S029, S035, S036, S044)
- **Dated:** estate exclusion $22 million for a couple as of 2018-08-20 (S036); about $22.8 million as of 2019-06-19 (S016); "20 some odd million" as of 2020-01-17 (S027). He says the exemption could go away (S016). As of 2020-12-10 he warns of proposals to cut it and end the step-up (S055).

## 19. The money-out ladder: salary, distribution, loan
- **What it is:** How to take money from the business without commingling.
- **Parts:** Salary for living money, kept reasonable (what you would pay someone else to do your job, S021, S023); in an S corporation take the rest as distribution; in a partnership only distributions; in a C corporation mostly salary; a loan from the company against a line of credit, with a quarterly journal entry (S021, S023); never pay personal costs from the business; expense reports for personal payment of business costs (S023). Borrowing is not income (S042).
- **When to use:** Founder pays themselves or family from the business account.
- **How to score:** 5 if salary is documented as replacement cost, distributions follow, no personal spend runs through the company; 1 if personal spend is booked as business.
- **Quote:** "do not ever, ever, ever use business money to pay for personal expenses" (S023)
- **Quote:** "What's reasonable is what would you pay somebody else to do your job?" (S021)
- **Sources:** 4 (S021, S023, S042, S053)
- **Conflict flagged (do not pick one):** S021 (2019-09-13) says a salary no more than half of what you take out is OK, from his experience with the IRS, "not a statute"; S023 (2019-10-24) says minimize salary but keep it reasonable; S053 (2019-12-20) says "Maybe you need to pay yourself wages" so the 20% deduction wage limit can be met. Different goals, never reconciled.

## 20. Two lines of defense: insurance first, entities second
- **What it is:** The goal is to avoid the lawsuit, not win it. Insurance and an umbrella first, because the insurer's lawyers are the real value; an LLC second; stay a harder target than the next person.
- **Parts:** Hold a living trust over LLCs; keep books and formalities or a judge ignores the LLC; the LLC is a lock on the door, not a vault; free-and-clear property is fully exposed to guest suits (S044); never put rental real estate in a corporation (S044).
- **When to use:** Any guest-facing or tenant-facing business.
- **How to score:** 5 if umbrella and liability coverage are in place and entities are maintained; 2 if only an LLC.
- **Quote:** "if I had to choose insurance and LLC, I would choose insurance." (S044)
- **Quote:** "you don't have to outrun the bear, you just have to outrun your buddy" (S009)
- **Sources:** 4 (S009, S023, S044, S015)
- **Dated:** as of 2022-02-10 he says states such as Tennessee make the LLC or limited partnership route harder (S044). Tennessee is the user's state; no recorded detail beyond that remark.

## 21. Loophole versus intended incentive
- **What it is:** A loophole is an unintended consequence. An incentive is designed on purpose. He uses the word to say that using an incentive is not cheating, and that a provision in the code is by definition not a loophole.
- **Parts:** Ask whether Congress meant it; stay inside the four corners of the law; the IRS cannot rewrite the law (S041).
- **When to use:** A founder or advisor calls a strategy shady.
- **How to score:** If the provision is in the code and used as designed, treat it as an incentive. If it works only because of an oversight, treat it as risk.
- **Quote:** "Loopholes are inadvertent tax benefits." (S001)
- **Quote:** "It's a code section. By definition it's not a loophole." (S041)
- **Sources:** 6 (S001, S022, S024, S030, S041, S044)
- **Dated:** as of 2023-06-23 conservation easements and captive insurance were under IRS challenge; he says they are in the code (S041). His guest advised erring toward safety; he did not endorse it. Treat that as an open dispute, not a settled one.

## 22. Year-end and bracket comparison
- **What it is:** Two goals: this year, and next. Compare this year's rate to next year's before deferring. Plan during the year, not in December.
- **Parts:** Year-end five (S053): compute where you stand, prepay a few months of expenses, donate appreciated stock, set up retirement plans, pay your children. Entities and gifts must be set up before year end (S055). Cash in hand beats a tax benefit (S053).
- **When to use:** October to December, or at the start of a year.
- **How to score:** Deferral is right only when next year's rate is not higher. His example: 30% this year versus 40% next year on $100,000 costs $10,000 (S055).
- **Quote:** "I'm not a fan of deferral, postponing taxes to a later year, if there's something I can do to permanently benefit this year, and lower taxes next year." (S055)
- **Sources:** 6 (S002, S012, S028, S038, S053, S055)
- **Tension:** S012 (2019-03-14) says "the year end is the worst time in the world to be doing tax planning" and use it only for projections, while S053 and S055 (2019-12-20, 2020-12-10) give year-end moves. Read as: plan all year, execute the leftovers by December. Inferred from S012, S053, S055.

## 23. The 20% pass-through deduction
- **What it is:** A deduction of 20% of qualified business income, created in the December 2017 law because corporations got a rate cut and small business did not. Includes rental income per S053.
- **Parts:** Specified service businesses lose it above an income threshold; wage limit of 50% of wages above it; a de minimis rule lets a service business with under 10% of revenue from the service side ignore it (S038); a retirement plan can pull income under the threshold (S053).
- **When to use:** Owner has pass-through income.
- **How to score:** Guidance only: if taxable income is under the threshold, expect the full deduction; above it, test wages and property.
- **Quote:** "you potentially get a deduction equal to 20% of your net business income." (S011)
- **Sources:** 9 (S002, S011, S012, S019, S024, S038, S042, S050, S053)
- **Dated:** as of 2019-03-06 thresholds $157,500 single and $315,000 married (2018 figures, S011); same figures as of 2019-12-20 (S053). As of 2019-05-21 he says no income limit for sales businesses, limits for CPA and physician services (S050). He read the 184 pages of new regulations live and said his reading was early (S038, 2018-10-11). Check the current status and expiry.

## 24. The $2,500 small-item rule
- **What it is:** Items costing under $2,500 are treated as materials and supplies and can be deducted when bought, if elected. A business under $25 million of gross revenue may stop keeping tax inventory.
- **Parts:** He applies it to rentals: 75 new washers and dryers each under $2,500 (S038). Elected on a filing, a one-time method change.
- **When to use:** Furnishing a rental, repairs, supplies.
- **How to score:** Guidance: per-item cost under the threshold and a documented election.
- **Quote:** "the rule now is that if your inventory items are less than $2,500, you don't have to keep inventory." (S038)
- **Sources:** 4 (S011, S024, S038, S050)
- **Dated:** as of 2018-10-11 (S038) and 2019-03-06 (S011). He says CPAs told him "you can't" in every city and he replied "that's what the law says" (S038). S050 (2019-05-21) records he was nearly alone talking about it, and a CPA warned of a 40% penalty; he researched four hours and found support. He says to treat it as aggressive until verified (note author reading). No recorded position that this covers furniture in a leased arbitrage unit; inferred from S038 only that furnishings under the threshold are the type of item he means.

## 25. Fund the trust and plan for peace
- **What it is:** A living trust only works if title moves into it. Estate planning is for family peace and control, not mainly for estate tax.
- **Parts:** Retitle assets by deed; match business assets to the children who work in it and use life insurance to even it out (S027); the trust owns the LLCs (S009); the revocable trust gives no asset protection (S009); permanent life insurance in an irrevocable trust (S049); the CPA and the estate attorney must talk (S016).
- **When to use:** Founder owns anything that would be hard to untangle at death or incapacity.
- **How to score:** 5 if assets are retitled; 1 if the document exists but the title never moved.
- **Quote:** "he had this great plan and he just had not executed the plan" (S027)
- **Sources:** 6 (S008, S009, S016, S027, S036, S049)
- **Dated:** as of 2019-12 he calls the change to inherited IRA rules a major one that escaped the news (S027, 2020-01-17); the details were the lawyer's. Whole life as a tax-free cash account is in S004 (2018-09-26) and S049 (2019-04-11), both with a friend selling the product, and he says it is "not for everyone" (S049).
