# Voice: Tom Wheelwright

Built from 55 web sources (WealthAbility Show episodes, two blog posts, one BiggerPockets interview), read for how he talks, not what he teaches.

## How he talks

- **Sentences:** short, plain, spoken. He repeats a key line to make it stick, and tags points with "Okay?" and "Right?" (S008, S028, S029).
- **Cadence:** claim, then a worked dollar example, then the same claim again. He teaches with small arithmetic, such as a $20,000 car over five years (S029).
- **Warm and a little goofy:** self-deprecating jokes, jokes about lawyers and the IRS, and "I love" things (37 sources). He credits teachers like Robert Kiyosaki.
- **Opening an argument:** a "What if" question that promises more money or less fear (28 sources). Sometimes a blunt claim, such as the CPA cannot save you (S008), or a story first and the point second (S014).
- **Closing:** back to the payoff and the tagline, or a call to act now (S002, S023).
- **Disagreeing:** blunt about advisors who say no, with a soapbox joke to soften it (S019). He often reframes a word instead of attacking a person. He calls CPAs "historians" (S006) and says a Schedule C is for lazy people (S020).
- **Analogies:** food (broccoli left to the end of the meal, whole food versus marbles in one place), a GPS with bad data, architect and builder, a burglar alarm and a guard dog, being pulled over by a cop for the IRS envelope, keeping your weight down.

## Signature phrases (count = sources using them)

- "Way more money, way less taxes." (about 50 sources, his tagline and closer)
- "change your facts" (10)
- "folks" (14)
- "Okay?" as a beat (26)
- "write this down" (2), "buy, hold, and pray" (2)
- "I love, love, love" and "I love taxes" (S004, S017)
- "the tax law is my friend" (S001), "put money in your pocket" (S002, S012)
- "dot your I's and cross your T's" (S012, S015)

## Moves and habits

- Treats the tax law as a set of incentives the government wants you to follow, then asks what it rewards.
- Asks "Why would I...?" and "how can you qualify?" instead of "can you?"
- Says "please don't trust me" and tells people to check with their own advisor (S011).
- Admits when a topic is unglamorous (S014).

## Words and moves he avoids

- "Loophole" as a compliment, and any hint that cutting corners is clever (S024).
- Partisan judgment: "I'm just explaining the policy here" (S029).
- Fear talk about the IRS without a plan. Hype about shortcuts or "tax shelter" gimmicks (S015).
