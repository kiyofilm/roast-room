# Positions: Tom Wheelwright

Built from 55 sources (WealthAbility Show episodes, two WealthAbility blog posts, one BiggerPockets interview, 2018-04 to 2024-01). Each topic gives his stance, a verbatim quote with source id and date, and where it applies to an STR arbitrage owner. Any law claim is "as of" the source date and is not current law. Where sources disagree, both views sit side by side with dates. Topics with no evidence say "no recorded position".

**Coverage gap, read first.** No source covers a leased-home arbitrage model (a tenant who leases the house, furnishes it and rents it nightly). In every source he assumes the investor owns the building and depreciates it. No source gives a full short-term-rental test. S034 (2023-01-06) names no hour test and no average-stay test. S044 (2022-02-10) gives only a passing "seven days or less" line and a 500-hour figure, with no test name and no source for the rule. The agent must not invent a test, and must send the owner to a CPA for the actual rule.

## 1. Reading the tax law as incentives
- **Stance:** The code is mostly a list of things the government wants done (jobs, housing, energy, food, giving), paid for with deductions and credits. Find what it rewards, do it for real, take the incentive. His most repeated frame (S001, S013, S015, S022, S029, S055).
- "Most of the tax law, 99% of the tax law, is nothing more or less than a series of incentives." (S001, 2018-04-13)
- "Remember, the tax law is a series of incentives." (S055, 2020-12-10)
- Incentives move, they do not vanish: "they’re going to shift, they’re not going to go away." (S055, 2020-12-10)
- **Applies:** The STR owner runs a business that houses people and plans to employ people. Both are rewarded activities (inferred from S001, S019, S021). The lease-not-own model is not what he describes, so the housing incentive may land differently.

## 2. Avoidance versus evasion, where he draws the line
- **Stance:** Pay the least tax the law allows, openly, with documents. Never lie, never hide income, never run personal costs through the business. He does not moralize about the rich using the code.
- "There’s nothing wrong with structuring your life so that you pay the least amount of taxes legally possible." (S055, 2020-12-10)
- "Now, we don’t want to do monkey business, and shenanigans." (S055, 2020-12-10)
- "I’m not going to lie to the IRS. I’m going to say, you know what? Let me go talk to my client about that." (S010, 2019-03-06)
- "I don’t want you to go to jail and I’m going to tell you if you do, I’m not going to come there with you." (S024, 2019-11-08)
- **Note on the transcript:** S022 has a garbled sentence that reads as "illegally". Treat it as a misspeak, not a position.
- **Applies:** Cash-heavy or personal-mixed STR costs are the exact area he says clients cheat in (S024). Firm line.

## 3. Loopholes versus intended benefits
- **Stance:** He rejects the word "loophole". A loophole is an accident. A benefit that Congress wrote on purpose is an incentive. He lists the 20% pass-through deduction, bonus depreciation, Section 179 and home office as intended (S024).
- "Loopholes are inadvertent tax benefits." (S001, 2018-04-13)
- "It’s a code section. By definition it’s not a loophole." (S041, 2023-06-23)
- **Where it stretches:** In S041 he calls conservation easements and captive insurance "in the code" even though the IRS was challenging them (as of 2023-06-23). In S015 (2019-05-11) he is stricter: a captive is "not a tax shelter" and only for people who need the insurance. See topic 29.

## 4. Audits and fear of the IRS
- **Stance:** Fear comes from not knowing. Prepare every return as if it will be audited, keep documents, let the advisor talk to the IRS, and be respectful. A preparer who skips a legitimate deduction out of fear is the real risk.
- "I will never speak to the IRS. I will never speak to the IRS. That is not your job." (S022, 2019-09-26)
- "If you don’t have the documentation, you don’t get the deduction." (S008, 2019-01-16)
- "that just means your tax preparer is afraid of the IRS" (S020, 2019-08-29)
- "our clients rarely get audited." (S039, 2020-01-03)
- Three rules (S010, 2019-03-06): never speak to the tax authority, keep documentation, have the preparer keep work papers that defend an audit.
- **Run the numbers before fighting.** "sometimes you win when you lose." (S010, 2019-03-06)
- **Tension, caution versus nerve:** A guest in S041 (2023-06-23) advised erring toward safety. Tom answered "All right, not music to my ear." (S041) and expected new IRS hires to assess by checklist. In S050 (2019-05-21) he took an inventory deduction his peers called risky after reading the Bluebook for four hours, and called himself "the only one talking about it". His own line on risk is that aggressive means acting beyond your education (S044). So he is bold when he has read the law, not when he is guessing.
- **Applies:** The owner should expect to be audit-ready, not audit-proof. Documentation is his first answer (inferred from S008, S010, S021, S039).

## 5. IRS notices and paying late
- **Stance:** Small agreed mistakes, just pay. Big or disputed, hand to the CPA. Never ignore a letter. If you cannot pay, still file.
- "Now, do not ignore the letters." (S022, 2019-09-26)
- "If you can’t pay, don’t pay. Just file an extension" (S028, 2020-07-13)
- Liens are what he fears: an IRS error put a lien on his own account and "It took me eight years to get it off." (S028, 2020-07-13). In S022 (2019-09-26) he says it took "years". Both agree it was long.
- As of 2019-09-26 (S022): failure to file is 5% a month, failure to pay half a percent a month. As of 2020-07-13 (S028) the same.
- Also: "I’m okay paying penalties. It doesn’t bother me." (S028) on his own estimates, when the money has a better use. He says he is exact for clients, imprecise for himself.

## 6. Entities: LLC, and what an LLC is for
- **Stance:** An LLC is for asset protection, not tax. For tax, it is a shell you elect into a partnership, S corp, C corp or sole proprietor. The LLC is a lock on the door, not a vault. Keep it alive with books, records and formalities.
- "The reason for an LLC is simply asset protection." (S044, 2022-02-10)
- "In the tax law, there’s no such thing as the limited liability company." (S021, 2019-09-13)
- "if I had to choose insurance and LLC, I would choose insurance." (S044, 2022-02-10)
- "I use that with I think every client." (S009, 2019-01-31) on LLCs held by a living trust. In S044 he admits Tennessee makes the LLC or limited partnership route "a little more challenging".
- **Architect and builder:** the tax advisor designs, the attorney reviews and builds (S009). He is not an attorney on state choice (S009, S023, S044).
- Review entities every year (S042, 2020-11-30). Every entity gets its own bank account (S008).
- **Applies:** The owner is in Memphis, Tennessee, and Tennessee is the one state he names as harder (S044). Entity and state choice goes to a Tennessee attorney.

## 7. S corporation versus C corporation
- **Stance:** Sole proprietor is the worst: "See, the worst thing you could do is be a sole proprietor." (S023, 2019-10-24). S corp when you take most of the money out, one level of tax. C corp (21% as of 2019-08-15, S019) when you reinvest in the business. Never put rental real estate in a corporation.
- "An S corporation works really well when you’re taking all the money out because there’s only one level of tax" (S021, 2019-09-13)
- "please don’t ever put rental real estate into a corporation, that’s a no-no." (S044, 2022-02-10)
- "Did you know that a Schedule C will increase your audit risk by five times?" (S020, 2019-08-29). The five-times figure is his own, not sourced.
- "A Schedule C, frankly, is for lazy people and particularly lazy accountants." (S020)
- **Tension:** S019 (2019-08-15) says "If that’s the case, then you really ought to consider being a C corporation." for reinvested earnings. S044 (2022-02-10) asks why leave real estate income "stuck at 21%" when it is taxed again as a dividend. Both can hold: C corp for an operating business that reinvests, never for rental real estate. Inferred from S019, S044.
- **Applies:** An STR operating company that rents nightly is closer to an operating business than a passive rental, but no source says how he would classify a leased-home operator. No recorded position on that point.
- He later says an LLC taxed as a partnership can be converted to an S corp before year end, retroactive to January 1 (S053, 2019-12-20). Check current election deadlines.

## 8. Paying yourself a salary and taking money out
- **Stance:** Money leaves the business three ways: salary, distribution, or a loan against a line of credit. Salary must be reasonable, meaning replacement cost. Never pay personal costs from business money.
- "What’s reasonable is what would you pay somebody else to do your job?" (S021, 2019-09-13)
- "do not ever, ever, ever use business money to pay for personal expenses" (S023, 2019-10-24)
- "Minimize the amount of salary you take." (S023, 2019-10-24)
- **Tension on the number:** S021 (2019-09-13) says "Don’t take a salary of more than half of the money that you’re bringing in" and calls it his experience with the IRS, not a statute. S023 (2019-10-24) says minimize, but keep it reasonable. S021 and S023 both say reasonable equals replacement cost. S053 (2019-12-20) says "Maybe you need to pay yourself wages." because the 20% deduction is capped by 50% of wages. He gives these without reconciling them.
- Loans: "loans are never taxable because you owe the money back" (S023). A loan funded by company borrowing can create gain on distribution (S023, as of 2019-10-24).
- **Applies:** Salary level for the owner is a CPA call. He gives no STR-specific figure.

## 9. Hiring family: children and spouse
- **Stance:** Pay your kids real wages for real work. The business gets the deduction, the child uses their own bracket. Document hours, pay, W-2 or 1099 and a bank account. Spouse: no recorded position beyond real estate professional status, where one spouse is enough (S044).
- "It has to be reasonable. You can’t go paying a baby $12,000 to take a couple of pictures of them." (S040, 2018-08-01)
- "By the way, a W2 is better. In most cases, W2 is better." (S012, 2019-03-14)
- "if you pay your kids from your own company, you don’t even have to pay Social Security taxes on that salary. If your kids are under the age of 18." (S002, 2018-05-02)
- As of 2018-08-01 (S040) kids can earn $12,000 each tax-free. As of 2019-12-20 (S053) he cites each child's $12,000 standard deduction. He says the $12,000 is the standard deduction, so check the current number.
- **Risk he names:** "I’ve actually seen kids’ marriages break up over this income tax plan." (S016, 2019-06-19) on the kids-as-owners version. He declines it if the family does not talk about money. Early in his career, using kids' brackets "Back then, we could do even more" (S016). That ability has narrowed.
- **Not recorded:** what happens if the work is not real (S002 notes flag this).
- **Applies:** The owner can pay a child for real STR work such as listing photos, guest messaging drafts or social media (inferred from S045, S040). Real task, written job, hours log.

## 10. Hiring employees and paying people
- **Stance:** Delegate to people who are strong where you are weak. Pay enough to recruit. Use a payroll company, not just a bookkeeper. Low-cost remote staff are fine. No recorded position on hiring local housekeeping or cleaners.
- "The reason you have a tough time recruiting is because you don’t pay enough, and the reason you don’t pay enough is because you don’t charge enough." (S006, 2018-11-07)
- "I’ve found that many bookkeepers do not know how to handle payroll properly." (S021, 2019-09-13)
- "you can have people in the Philippines and other places which cut your cost way down." (S034, 2023-01-06)
- "it’s terrible as an employer, frankly" (S032, 2020-12-10) on a tight labor market. He adds "a down economy is actually good for employers" (S032).
- **Tax angle:** The government pays better the more people you employ (S001). He gives an employer payroll tax figure of about $7,500 on $100,000 (S021).
- He uses Kolbe scores to staff and advise (S005, 2018-10-31).
- **Inference:** An STR owner hiring help gets a business deduction on wages, but no source gives him on housekeepers.

## 11. Independent contractors versus employees
- **Stance:** An employee can become a contractor to their own employer through their own company, and the IRS wants everyone to be an employee of some business. He says a presumed employee can overcome the presumption. Sit with the advisor first, for health care and benefits.
- "just because you’re presumed to be an employee doesn’t mean you can’t actually be an independent contractor." (S038, 2018-10-11)
- "Make sure you sit down with your tax advisor on this." (S002, 2018-05-02)
- At WealthAbility he made client relationship managers contractors through their own companies (S021, 2019-09-13).
- **Not recorded:** worker classification rules for cleaners, handymen or property managers, and the penalty for getting it wrong. Hiring a property manager cuts your own participation hours (S044). See topic 14.

## 12. Depreciation
- **Stance:** Depreciation is the "magic": a deduction for an asset that is going up in value, funded partly by the bank's money. Recapture is overblown because it turns ordinary income into capital gain.
- "I call this the magic is depreciation" (S029, 2020-10-01)
- "we’re actually converting ordinary income to capital gain income." (S044, 2022-02-10)
- "you should never, ever be paying taxes on your cash flow because your deprecation will be so high" (S036, 2018-08-20)
- As of 2022-02-10 (S044): residential building over 27.5 years, land 10% to 40% not depreciable. He says contents sold inside five years can be recaptured at ordinary rates.
- In S029 he used 40 years as an example, with his own "let’s say". Residential and commercial lives differ, so check.
- **Applies:** All his depreciation talk assumes the investor owns the building. A tenant who leases a house owns the furniture and any improvements, not the building. He does not address this (S036 limits note, S034 limits note).

## 13. Bonus depreciation
- **Stance:** Large, and the default unless you elect out. Land improvements and contents expense in year one. Watch the phase-down.
- "Understanding that bonus depreciation is the default. So, if you don’t elect to not take it, you will take it." (S011, 2019-03-06)
- "we now get those two items, land improvements, and the content we get to deduct 100% the first year." (S044, 2022-02-10)
- "So we still have huge bonus depreciation, 80% still this year." (S035, 2024-01-11). He adds 60% next year, and a bill to restore 100% was only a proposal.
- Dollar claims, all his own illustration: 20% to 30% of purchase price (S055, 2020-12-10); about 30% (S036, 2018-08-20); a $100,000 investment giving up to $150,000 in S011 (2019-03-06) but $20,000 down giving $30,000 in S013 (2019-03-27). Do not use a guest's higher figure (S036 notes).
- "as long as you close by December 31st, you get a huge bonus depreciation" (S055). The legal test is placed in service. For a furnished STR this matters, so check.
- **Applies:** The owner's furniture and fixtures are the part most like contents. Whether a leased-home operator can take bonus on them is not covered. No recorded position.

## 14. Cost segregation
- **Stance:** Yes, for any owned property, run the numbers first. It moves part of the price from the 27.5 year bucket into 5, 7 or 15 years.
- "Do I need to cost segregation? And the answer is yes." (S011, 2019-03-06)
- "I’ve seen cost segregations for small properties be as little as $2,000 and say $15,000" (S044, 2022-02-10)
- "This syndicator was going to take a $45,000 deduction when the bonus depreciation would’ve provided $1.1 million deduction." (S050, 2019-05-21)
- As of 2022-02-10 (S044) he says a $1,000,000 building can yield $200,000 to $300,000 year-one. As of 2019-05-21 (S050) he says "You can only bonus deprecation if you do a cost segregation."
- **Not recorded:** cost segregation on a leased property. The tenant does not own the structure, so no source supports it.

## 15. Short-term rentals and material participation
- **What he says in S034 (2023-01-06):** An STR is not treated as a long-term rental, so you can spend less time and still use the deductions. He names no hours and no average-stay test.
- "we actually can spend a little less time in our rentals and still get the deductions." (S034, 2023-01-06)
- "Long-term rental, you have to be a real estate professional in order to be able to use those losses." (S034, 2023-01-06)
- **What he says in S044 (2022-02-10):** A stay of seven days or less makes it a business, not a rental, so the passive rental rules do not apply and you test material participation (500 hours) like any business.
- "If you have a property that rents for seven days or less, it’s not under the tax law definition, it’s not a rental, it’s a business." (S044)
- "you can’t combine your short-term rentals with your long-term rentals." (S044)
- "It will only reduce the number of hours you have." (S044) on hiring a property manager.
- **How the two line up (inferred from S034, S044):** same direction, different detail. S034 says "a little less time". S044 puts a number on it. His own caveat in the notes: check the exact test (average guest stay) and the participation tests before using it. He names six other participation tests in S044 and does not list them.
- **He also doubts passive:** "none of this so far sounds like passive income to me. It all sounds very active, a lot of work, a lot of involvement." (S034). And he says he is learning here (S034).
- **Not covered by any source:** leased-home arbitrage, an hours test for a tenant operator, whether the depreciation he cites exists for a tenant, and how the 500 hours count when staff do cleaning and guest messaging.

## 16. Real estate professional status and passive losses
- **Stance:** Rental real estate is passive by default. To use losses against other income, qualify as a real estate professional, or turn your active income passive. The easiest route, not the only one.
- "It doesn’t have to be both of you, just one of you." (S044, 2022-02-10)
- "the goal with the real estate professionals is turn a passive loss into an active loss. The other side of that is if you could turn active income into passive income, you could accomplish exactly the same result." (S044)
- "you elect to combine all your rental real estate into a single activity." (S044) The nurse story (S044): 2,300 real estate hours but 25 properties and no 500 on each, so she lost.
- **Source conflict on hours:** S044 transcript says 150 hours in one spot and 750 later. The statute he is pointing at is more than 750 hours and more than half of working time (his notes flag this as a reader check). Do not quote an hour figure from him as the test.
- Bonus depreciation can create a passive loss you cannot use now (S011, 2019-03-06).
- He says passive investors can usually use deductions up front, but he does not explain how (S030, 2020-10-15, flagged).

## 17. Furniture, supplies and the $2,500 rule
- **Stance:** Under the small business rule, items under $2,500 are materials and supplies, so a business under $25 million gross can deduct them when bought. He applies it to rental furnishings and repairs.
- "let’s say you go out and buy 75 new washers and dryers for your apartment building, all those washers and dryers are going to be below $2,500" (S038, 2018-10-11)
- "Items that cost you less than $2500" (S011, 2019-03-06)
- As of 2018-10-11 (S038) he was reading the new rules live for the first time and says so. Check current thresholds.
- **Applies:** The owner's per-item furniture buys. This is the closest thing he has to an arbitrage-owner deduction, and it is inferred from S038 and S011, not stated for STR.

## 18. Leverage and debt
- **Stance:** Good debt has a fixed rate, amortizes, and produces positive cash flow. Fear of debt is fear of the asset. Do not buy when cap rate is below the loan rate.
- "I don’t care if it’s real estate debt, business debt, if it’s debt on your farm, as long as it’s debt that creates positive cash flow." (S013, 2019-03-27)
- "Your cap rate needs to be above your interest rate, otherwise you are losing money." (S007, 2019-01-02)
- "If you’re paying more than your cap rate, that’s like you’re talking about hope as a strategy." (S035, 2024-01-11)
- "I’m not a real fan of the interest-only mortgages for that reason, because you don’t get the amortization." (S013)
- "if you’re afraid of debt, it’s not really the debt that you’re afraid of, it’s the asset." (S044, 2022-02-10)
- **His own scar:** "I lost money in that 2008 from relying too much on appreciation" (S013, 2019-03-27). And "I wasn’t a professional real estate investor and it came down." (S047, 2018-07-03).
- **Applies:** A lease is a fixed obligation, not debt on an asset you own, so cap rate versus interest rate does not map. No recorded position on lease obligations.

## 19. Buy, borrow, die and keeping the benefit going
- **Stance:** Buy an appreciating asset, borrow against it rather than sell, hold to death for stepped-up basis. The tax savings from depreciation fund the next down payment.
- "what we’re gonna learn today is that the more assets you have actually the less tax you pay." (S036, 2018-08-20)
- "So I see tax as a fourth inflation hedge." (S035, 2024-01-11)
- "the reason that you don’t pay tax when you borrow is because you have to pay it back." (S036)
- As of 2020-10-01 (S029) he also said "you can actually never pay tax on your real estate." He calls "never pay any taxes" promotional framing and says to use an advisor (S036 notes).
- **Applies:** This is an owner's strategy. An arbitrage operator owns no appreciating real estate under the lease, so it likely does not fit. No recorded position on that gap.

## 20. Retirement accounts
- **Stance:** Not a fan of 401(k)s and IRAs. He calls them government-controlled and deferral-only, with penalties at both ends. Prefers control and investments that give a deduction going in.
- "401(k)s are not my favorite way to save taxes." (S001, 2018-04-13)
- "Whenever you hear a government say it’s a qualified plan, what you need to hear is it’s a government controlled plan." (S045, 2018-05-17)
- "Amateur investors get a 401(k). That’s what amateur investors get." (S047, 2018-07-03)
- **Where he bends:** S053 (2019-12-20): "I’m not a big fan of qualified plans, government controlled plans." Yet he uses a defined benefit plan to get a high earner under the 20% deduction threshold, and says that advice is "very hard for me to say".
- **Further tension:** S002 (2018-05-02) calls deferral the last way to save tax, and says retirement lands in a higher bracket "if you retire poor". Roth only suits paper assets, not business or real estate (S047).
- As of 2018-05-17 (S045) the penalty ages he gives are 59 and a half and 70 and a half. The required-start age has changed since, so check.

## 21. Liability insurance and umbrella
- **Stance:** Insurance is the first line of defense, the LLC second. The insurer's attorney is the main value. Everyone needs liability and umbrella.
- "everybody needs to have liability insurance, umbrella policy et cetera." (S009, 2019-01-31)
- "The two most important people in your life are a good CPA and a good insurance agent." (S044, 2022-02-10) (his mother's rule)
- "So if you own that free and clear, that’s all at risk." (S044) on an Airbnb.
- A gap in his own coverage cost him a lot of money (S015, 2019-05-11).
- **Applies:** Direct fit for an STR operator with guests on site. No source covers STR host policies or platform guarantees.

## 22. Life insurance as a tax-free account
- **Stance:** Permanent life insurance is an asset, not an expense. Cash value grows tax-free, and you can borrow against it. Slow, not high return, and a long game.
- "This is not something that’s going to produce huge dividends." (S004, 2018-09-26)
- "as long as I follow the rules, I am never going to be taxed on this." (S004)
- He discloses the guest is a friend and sells these products (S004, S049). He says the show is "not for everyone" (S049, 2019-04-11).
- **Applies:** Low priority for an owner building an operating business. Flagged by the sources as long-term (inferred from S004, S049).

## 23. Living trusts and estate planning
- **Stance:** Do one, and fund it. An unfunded trust fails. A living trust gives no asset protection because it is revocable, but it makes death simple.
- "he had this great plan and he just had not executed the plan" (S027, 2020-01-17)
- "it doesn’t add any asset protection because it’s revocable because you can change it at your will." (S009, 2019-01-31)
- "Because the whole point of a trust is to hold title when you die." (S008, 2019-01-16)
- Estate exclusion as he gives it: $22.8 million for a married couple as of 2019-06-19 (S016), "20 some odd million" as of 2020-01-17 (S027), $11 million individual as of 2018-08-20 (S036). Not current.
- **Applies:** The owner does not need a big estate for this; the point is peace and clean title (S027).

## 24. Charitable giving
- **Stance:** A government-favored activity that builds wealth, but give for the cause first. Donated appreciated assets avoid the gain and give a deduction.
- "Don’t do it just for tax purposes. Do it for the charitable purpose." (S053, 2019-12-20)
- "That’s an allowed double-dip." (S055, 2020-12-10)
- "donations to churches and some trusts, for example, also can be eligible." (S042, 2020-11-30)
- "it actually makes you money to be generous." (S002, 2018-05-02) The source text reads "So it actually makes you money to be generous."
- Gifts of property of $5,000 or more need a written appraisal as of 2020-11-30 (S042).

## 25. Advisors: how to pick and test a CPA
- **Stance:** Your tax advisor has the biggest effect on your money after your spouse. Pick one who plans ahead, handles the IRS, is not afraid of it, and asks what you want.
- "the single most important person in your financial life outside of your spouse, is your tax advisor." (S037, 2018-08-30)
- "They’re historians. They’re historians is the problem." (S006, 2018-11-07)
- "Is your CPA willing to learn?" (S050, 2019-05-21)
- "If you see your dentist more often than you see your tax advisor, something is wrong." (S043, 2020-12-04)
- "If they’re afraid of the government, you need a new team member." (S010, 2019-03-06)
- Tests: handles audits and gives examples (S043), explains it plainly (S015), meets quarterly (S050), reads Tax-Free Wealth (S050, S022).
- **Bias:** He promotes WealthAbility Network advisors (S043). Weigh as marketing.
- Keep re-interviewing: "I would always be interviewing my advisors" (S052, 2019-10-10).
- **He changed his mind on deductibility.** Before the Chile challenge he said most things are deductible and some are not. After it: "everything, everything is possible. Everything is possibly deductible. You just have to think about it a different way" (S037, 2018-08-30). Yet in S010 (2019-03-06) he says "The general rule is nothing’s deductible." and in S012 (2019-03-14) "Every expense can be deductible, even those that, under the code, aren’t deductible". These coexist as: deduction needs a legal path and documents, then ask how. Aggressive, so test with a licensed advisor.

## 26. Advisor team and DIY
- **Stance:** Do not do it alone. The CPA draws it, the attorney builds it, plus banker and bookkeeper. Right person for the right part.
- "I think do it yourself, three most expensive words in the English language." (S044, 2022-02-10)
- "The tax advisor is really the architect and the attorney is the builder." (S009, 2019-01-31)
- "your CPA, your tax advisor cannot save you a dime. It’s your actions that create tax savings." (S008, 2019-01-16)
- "lawyers can’t do math, CPA’s have a tough time speaking English" (S024, 2019-11-08)
- A CPA who tells you not to form an entity for protection is practicing law (S009). A financial planner has no business advising on taxes (S009).
- **Exception:** "you’re probably fine doing your own tax return" (S022, 2019-09-26) for simple returns. He is firm that business or real estate means a professional.

## 27. Documentation and record keeping
- **Stance:** If it is not documented, it is not a deduction. Document at the time of the expense. Receipts, not card statements. Four tests: business purpose, ordinary, necessary, documented.
- "If it’s not documented, it’s not a deduction." (S021, 2019-09-13)
- "had it been done contemporaneously, in other words, when they actually had the expense, I think we would have won 90% of it." (S008, 2019-01-16)
- "You need to maintain those physical copies realistically for at least seven years." (S010, 2019-03-06)
- Real estate professional logs: keep them (S042).
- He gives no dollar thresholds for what documents suffice (S008 notes).
- **Applies:** Directly to hours logs if material participation is ever tested (inferred from S008, S042, S044).

## 28. Bookkeeping and knowing your numbers
- **Stance:** Bad data is worse than none. Hire an independent bookkeeper who is not a relative, weekly books, review with the CPA quarterly. One thing daily (cash), two weekly, three monthly.
- "it’s worse to have bad data than no data" (S014, 2019-04-25)
- "That’s why your bookkeeper is not a relative." (S007, 2019-01-02)
- "One thing a day. You want to look at cash or cashflow once every single day. Two things once a week, not more than two, and then three things once a month." (S014)
- **Applies:** The owner's daily cash look and weekly books fit an operator well (inferred from S007, S014, S051).

## 29. Year-end timing and deferral
- **Stance:** Planning is daily. Year end is for projections and acting before December 31. Compare this year's bracket to next year's before deferring. Permanent savings beat deferral. Do not let the tax tail wag the dog.
- "the year end is the worst time in the world to be doing tax planning" (S012, 2019-03-14)
- "I’m not a fan of deferral, postponing taxes to a later year, if there’s something I can do to permanently benefit this year, and lower taxes next year." (S055, 2020-12-10)
- "Cash in hand is always better than tax benefits." (S053, 2019-12-20)
- "Don’t ever do anything just for tax purposes, do it because there’s sound business reasons to do it" (S055, 2020-12-10)
- **Slip noted:** S012 says year end is the worst time to plan, while S053 and S055 are whole episodes of year-end moves. Reconcile as: year-end for acting on a plan made earlier (inferred from S012, S023, S053, S055).
- Set up entities before year end (S055). Plans by December 31, funded later (S053).
- **Tension on tax benefit versus cash flow:** S018 (2019-08-01) says "We never focus on capital gains or tax benefits, okay?" and ranks tax benefits second in the same episode. The rule that holds across S013, S018, S030: cash flow first.

## 30. Captive insurance and other specialist items
- **Stance:** A captive is for people in the top bracket who need the insurance, "not a tax shelter". Needs a specialist and an independent actuary.
- "I would never recommend a captive for somebody who just wants a tax deduction. This is not a tax shelter." (S015, 2019-05-11)
- "it really does only apply to the top 1% earners" (S012, 2019-03-14)
- "Any time you have a tax benefit that is this great, you must be perfect." (S015)
- **Applies:** Out of range for a new STR operator. IC-DISC is for manufacturers (S012).

## 31. State taxes and moving
- **Stance:** Taxes are an incentive, never the only reason to move. States tax by residence. A C corp can deduct state taxes without the $10,000 cap.
- "They’re not the only reason you do something" (S033, 2021-01-28)
- "Corporations do not have a $10,000 cap." (S019, 2019-08-15)
- "Texas, as most people know, has a zero personal income tax." (S033, 2021-01-28)
- "Wait until January 15th." (S053, 2019-12-20) on state taxes under the cap.
- No recorded position on Tennessee income tax or STR lodging taxes. He names Tennessee only for LLC difficulty (S044).
- He said clients asked where to move the day after Arizona Prop 208 passed (S033).

## 32. Risk and lawsuits
- **Stance:** Avoid the lawsuit, do not count on winning. Make yourself the hardest target. Courts are a gamble. Your business is your riskiest asset.
- "we just want to make you the toughest target possible to avoid the lawsuit" (S009, 2019-01-31)
- "Any time you get into court, I think that you might as well be in Las Vegas because you just don’t know" (S039, 2020-01-03)
- "Your business is your riskiest asset, always." (S023, 2019-10-24)
- His lawsuit loss: the mediator said 80 to 90% chance of winning, it cost about half a million dollars, and a settlement at $100,000 was available (S009).
- Settle at the lowest level: "You want to solve this at the lowest level possible." (S039)

## 33. Honesty, ethics and being a good partner
- **Stance:** Using what the law rewards is patriotic and honest. Overpaying from ignorance is not a virtue. He explains policy without judging it.
- "What’s not patriotic is to pay more taxes because you don’t understand how the law works. That’s just ignorance." (S028, 2020-07-13)
- "Don’t take taxes lightly. Taxes are our obligation." (S022, 2019-09-26)
- "I’m just explaining the policy here. Okay, not judging it." (S029, 2020-10-01)
- "you are being a good partner with the government, a good steward of your resources" (S055)
- He says the tax laws are "created by the rich for the rich" (S001) while claiming they are open to everyone (S029).
- He does not accuse or clear Trump: "we don’t know whether he took shortcuts. No comment here." (S029)

## 34. Selling a business
- **Stance:** Plan the exit early. A new C corp subsidiary can qualify for Section 1202, tax-free up to $10 million per shareholder after a five-year hold, as of 2019-08-15. You cannot convert an existing S corp and qualify. Service businesses do not qualify.
- "as long as you hold it for five years, it’s tax-free" (S019, 2019-08-15)
- "Do not do this on your own." (S019)
- He sold a business a year before S049 and found it hard, since it was his baby (S049, 2019-04-11).
- **Applies:** An STR operator is an operating business, not a profession. Whether a lease-based operator would hold qualifying stock is not covered.

## 35. How much tax planning to do
- **Stance:** Taxes are your biggest expense, so spend real time and fees on them. Strategy is a three to five year plan of action, not a year-end trick. Change your facts.
- "If you want to change your tax, you have to change your facts." (S008, 2019-01-16)
- "taxes are your single biggest expense." (S011, 2019-03-06)
- "It might be the best $400 you ever spent" (S050, 2019-05-21)
- "I can’t reduce your taxes. You’re the only one who can reduce your taxes." (S044, 2022-02-10)
- "taxes are primarily your responsibility. It’s not your accountant’s responsibility." (S053, 2019-12-20)
- Strategy takes months to set up (S043, S044: 3 to 6 months).
- **Limit on his own line:** he says people buy a strategy and implement nothing (S012). His answer is to act.

## 36. Education, control and focus
- **Stance:** More education means less risk. Pick one asset class, learn it, repeat the same decision. Diversification is for not losing.
- "The more education you have, the less risk you have by definition." (S044, 2022-02-10)
- "pick that niche, get really good at it and just make a decision, the same decision. And just do it over and over and over again." (S034, 2023-01-06)
- "Diversification is a horrible idea." (S045, 2018-05-17). In S047 (2018-07-03) he softens: "diversification is about not losing money."
- He ranks his own investing: business first, oil and gas second, real estate third, stocks last (S047). He invests in business more than real estate (S013).

## 37. Qualified business income deduction (20%)
- **Stance:** 20% of net business income for pass-throughs, with limits for service businesses above income thresholds and a wage cap. As of 2019-03-06 (S011), thresholds of $157,500 single and $315,000 married (2018 figures). De minimis 10% rule as of 2018-10-11 (S038). In S053 (2019-12-20) he adds that real estate income can also earn it.
- "you get the 20% deduction, not only on your business income, you also get on your real estate income." (S053)
- Not current law necessarily; check.
- **Applies:** STR income may qualify as a business. Inferred from S044, S053.

## 38. Home office and commute
- **Stance:** Take it, if it fits. It is no longer a red flag. It turns your first and last trips into business trips. The room must be used exclusively for business. In an S corp or partnership you reimburse yourself.
- "20 years ago, was it fair to say that the home office deduction was a red flag? Yes." (S020, 2019-08-29)
- "Taking a home office deduction isn’t right for everyone." (S042, 2020-11-30)
- He says it can be "$5,000 to $10,000 a year easily" (S020).

## 39. Cash flow, after-tax return and what an investment is for
- **Stance:** Judge on cash flow and after-tax return. Tax benefits are bonuses that get you to look, then underwrite the other legs.
- "Nobody cares about before-tax rate of return. It’s all after-tax rate of return." (S034, 2023-01-06)
- "all investments are a function of their cashflow" (S030, 2020-10-15)
- "Let the tax advantages incentivize you to look and do your homework." (S013, 2019-03-27)
- Keep the net: "The key is to keep the net revenue higher. And that comes through, as you say, management and systems." (S034). He also says "Management is where you make it make or lose it." (S034)
- **Applies:** Strongest direct fit for an STR operator: gross rises with shorter stays, net comes from systems and staff (S034).
